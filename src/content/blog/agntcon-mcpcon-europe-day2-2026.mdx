## AGNTCon + MCPCon Europe 2026: Day 2 — The Agentic Stack in Production

**Day 2 recap** — Stateless MCP goes to production, agent identity takes center stage, and the reality of multi-agent orchestration in the real world.

Day 2 at AGNTCon + MCPCon Europe opened with a clear theme: the agentic stack is no longer a vision — it's a production reality. Each session built on Day 1's announcements, diving deeper into the practical challenges of running agentic systems at scale.

![RAI Amsterdam auditorium on Day 2 of AGNTCon + MCPCon Europe 2026](/blog/events/agntcon-mcpcon-europe-2026/agntcon-2026-09.jpg)

*The RAI Amsterdam auditorium on Day 2 — the conversation shifted from "agentic AI works" to "agentic AI works reliably."*

## Keynote: Welcome — Angie Jones, VP, Agentic AI Foundation

**9:00 AM | Auditorium (Ground Floor)**

Day 2 kicked off with **Angie Jones** returning to the stage for the welcome keynote. If Day 1 was about proving that agentic AI works in production (81% of teams report production deployment), Day 2 was about proving it works **reliably** — across frameworks, models, and deployment targets.

Angie's message: the standards wars are over. MCP, A2A, Skills, and AGENTS.md are now the shared vocabulary of the agentic stack. The question is no longer which protocol to use, but how to make them work together.

## Keynote: Catch Them Early — Manik Surtani, Agentic AI Foundation

**9:07 AM | Auditorium (Ground Floor)**

Next up was **Manik Surtani**, introduced on his title slide as *CTO and co-founder, Agentic AI Foundation*. The [session abstract](https://agntconmcpconeu26.sched.com/list/descriptions/type/Keynote+Sessions) set out the problem: the foundation wanted to be the neutral referee for the agentic layer, then found it had inherited CNCF's entry bar, a bar built for a mature industry, while this field is only just over a year old.

![Title slide reading Catch Them Early, Manik Surtani, CTO and co-founder, Agentic AI Foundation, with Manik Surtani on the main stage at RAI Amsterdam](/blog/events/agntcon-mcpcon-europe-day2-2026/catch-them-early.jpg)

*Manik Surtani opening "Catch Them Early" on the Day 2 main stage.*

His "Why we started" slide asked **"Who keeps it open?"** It was about goose: the project "found its people", and then its community asked a question he "couldn't answer well". The slide's diagnosis: "Open code. Permissive licence. But the trademarks and the roadmap still belonged to one company."

The fix, a few minutes later, was **"Sandbox: a second door"**: a new first step in a **Sandbox → Growth → Impact** path. Projects are still judged on the same three axes (adoption, community, foundation commitment), and the bar rises at each step:

- **What it takes:** a working implementation, plus early interest or a credible thesis, and a named, active maintainer.
- **What you get:** infrastructure and a neutral flag. No funding, no marketing.
- **Reaching Growth:** production use by two unaffiliated organisations, commits from two organisations over six months, and a written plan that a Technical Committee (TC) sponsor accepts.

The AAIF's own [post on the Sandbox phase](https://aaif.io/blog/aaif-sandbox-phase), written by Manik Surtani and published on 1 September 2026, adds the timing. There's a six-month checkpoint, and a project has twelve months to apply for Growth. After that, the TC discusses archiving it. The post treats archival as part of the design, not as a failure.

**My take:** this reads like the CNCF Sandbox lesson applied a lot earlier. For platform teams the useful part is the signal. A Sandbox project has a named maintainer and a neutral home, and that's all it promises. Before you build on it, check its phase and whether it has reached the two-organisation production bar yet.

## Keynote: Getting to Stateless MCP — In Production

**9:19 AM | Auditorium (Ground Floor)**

**Shaun Smith** from **Hugging Face** — MCP and Transport Working Group Maintainer — delivered the keynote that many attendees had been waiting for: a deep dive into **stateless MCP in production**.

This directly follows yesterday's announcement that MCP is graduating to enterprise infrastructure with a stateless core, new extensions, and stronger authorization. Shaun walked through what the stateless transport actually means in practice:

- **No persistent sessions** — each MCP request is independent, enabling horizontal scaling without sticky sessions
- **Reduced memory footprint** — servers don't need to maintain connection state, cutting resource usage by up to 60%
- **Simplified failover** — stateless servers can be load-balanced and scaled independently
- **Clear migration path** — existing MCP implementations can adopt stateless mode with backward compatibility

Shaun demonstrated a production deployment handling 10,000+ concurrent agent sessions across 3 availability zones — something that was not possible with the session-based architecture MCP launched with.

![Slide titled Deployment Tips listing migrate to latest SDK versions, enable list caching, and stateless: do you need discover or subscribe, with the speaker on stage](/blog/events/agntcon-mcpcon-europe-day2-2026/deployment-tips.jpg)

*The "Deployment Tips" slide from the stateless MCP keynote.*

The practical checklist fit on one slide, **"Deployment Tips"**: migrate to the latest SDK versions, enable list caching, and for stateless deployments ask whether you really need `discover` or `subscribe`.

## Keynote: Three Doors to One Tool — MCP vs WebMCP vs CLI

**9:29 AM | Auditorium (Ground Floor)**

**Frédéric Barthelet** (CTO & Co-founder, **Alpic**) and **Dominic Farolino** (Software Engineer, **Google**) presented the third keynote: comparing three approaches to agent-to-environment connectivity.

| | MCP | WebMCP | CLI |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Structured tool calls | Web browsing automation | Direct command execution |
| **Use case** | Backend APIs, databases | Web applications, UIs | System administration |
| **Maturity** | Production-ready | Emerging | Mature |
| **Complexity** | Medium | High | Low |

The key insight: these three protocols aren't competitors — they're **complementary**. An agent navigating the web uses WebMCP to interact with browser-based applications, then calls MCP for structured backend access, and falls back to CLI for system-level operations.

Frédéric also discussed the announcement from yesterday's Alpic AI news: **AI assistants are becoming a new business channel**, with MCP running in production at consumer brands like PGA of America and AirHelp. The "three doors" framework helps teams choose the right connectivity model for each use case.

## Keynote: State of the Software Factory — Dex Horthy

**9:41 AM | Auditorium (Ground Floor)**

The last keynote of the morning came from **Dex Horthy** (*@dexhorthy* on his slides), with the footer *"state of the software factory"*. The [official schedule](https://agntconmcpconeu26.sched.com/list/descriptions/type/Keynote+Sessions) puts it on Day 2 at 9:41. I gave it a short paragraph in my [Day 1 recap](/blog/agntcon-mcpcon-europe-2026/), so this section covers only what the slides showed.

The first slides I photographed set up two warning signs:

- **Loop engineering.** A slide pairing two posts, one from Addy Osmani and one from Peter Steinberger, made the same point: stop prompting coding agents yourself and design the loops that prompt them.
- **The cost of speed.** Faros AI's *AI Engineering Report 2026*, ["The Acceleration Whiplash"](https://www.faros.ai/research/ai-acceleration-whiplash), was summarised on the slide as: "Engineering throughput is up. However, bugs, incidents, and rework are rising faster." The slide said it drew on two years of data from 22,000 developers.

Two diagrams followed:

- **"A Software Factory Circa 2022"**: users send complaints and feature requests to the CEO, product managers and engineers. That becomes "stuff to do" in Linear, Jira or another issue tracker. Then "someone builds the thing" with automated and manual testing, opens a pull request, passes CI/CD checks (unit tests, static scanning, security checks), a human tests and reviews the change, and it rolls out to production.
- **"The Agentic Software Factory"**: the same loop with "someone builds the thing" replaced by a stack of **orchestration, harness, sandbox, model**. Agentic testing (for example computer use) sits alongside automated testing, **agentic code review** and **agentic regression testing** sit next to the pull request, and a **monitoring** box feeds incidents back into the loop. The two human steps, testing and reviewing the change, are still there.

Then came **"enter SlopCodeBench"**. [SlopCodeBench](https://www.scbench.ai) is a community benchmark for coding agents. Its [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24755) (v2, May 2026) describes 36 problems and 196 checkpoints in which an agent keeps extending its own earlier solution as the specification changes. Besides pass rates, it measures two kinds of degradation: structural erosion (complexity piling up in a few functions) and verbosity (redundant code). The paper's abstract says no agent solved any problem end to end, the best agent passed 14.8% of checkpoints, and erosion rose in 77% of trajectories.

![SlopCodeBench slide titled Strict checkpoint pass rate, comparing live v1.0 campaign results with v2 paper results on a fixed 196-checkpoint denominator, with Dex Horthy pointing at the screen](/blog/events/agntcon-mcpcon-europe-day2-2026/slopcodebench-strict-checkpoint-pass-rate.jpg)

*Dex Horthy's "Strict checkpoint pass rate" slide: no model on it got past 16.3%.*

The **"Strict checkpoint pass rate"** slide had two panels:

| Full v1.0 campaigns (live) | Strict pass rate | V2 paper (fixed 196-checkpoint denominator) | Strict pass rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-6 Astra XHigh (32/196) | 16.3% | GPT-5.5 | 14.8% |
| GPT-5.6 SOL XHigh (28/196) | 14.3% | GPT-5.3 Codex | 11.2% |
| GLM 5.3 Max (24/192, provisional) | 12.5% | GPT-5.4 | 10.7% |
| | | Kimi K2.6 | 10.7% |
| | | Opus 4.6 | 9.7% |
| | | GPT-5.2 Codex | 9.7% |

The last slide I photographed, **"Problem to Solve"**, explained why a factory needs more than a better model. Every coding-agent session "starts cold". Users type the same instructions into session after session (one example was `bun --bun run test`). They keep reminding the agent of facts it can't get from the code, such as "the payments team owns that service, don't touch it". Nothing notices when "five engineers all told the agent the same thing" and turns that into shared, reusable steering, so new teammates don't benefit from what the team has already worked out.

**My take:** the agentic factory diagram is a CI/CD pipeline with the "builder" box swapped out. That's good news for platform teams, because the gates (tests, scanning, review, monitoring) are things we already know how to run. The SlopCodeBench numbers argue for keeping those gates strict. When even the top models pass well under a fifth of iterative checkpoints, a single green run tells you little. Steering that teams keep repeating belongs in the repository, in AGENTS.md, Skills and CI policy, where every session and every new teammate picks it up.

## Breakout: What *IS* an Agent's Identity?

**1:50 PM | Auditorium (Ground Floor)**

**Christian Posta** (Chief Architect, **Solo.io**) tackled one of the most fundamental questions in agentic AI: if agents have write access to systems, *who* is acting?

The session covered:
- How to establish agent identity in distributed systems
- The difference between **authentication** (who is this?) and **authorization** (what is this allowed to do?)
- Practical patterns for agent identity in Kubernetes, service meshes, and zero-trust architectures
- Integration with the Sovereign Trust Plane announced yesterday by Traefik Labs

## Breakout: Agents Talking to Agents

**3:40 PM | G102 + G103 (1st Floor)**

**Willem Berroubache** (Orange) presented on the reality of multi-agent orchestration in production. With **60% of teams** now running multi-agent systems (per the AAIF survey), the question is no longer whether agents can talk to each other — but whether they can do it **reliably**.

Key takeaways:
- **A2A** (Agent2Agent) provides the protocol, but **semantic alignment** is still a human problem
- **Tool consistency** across agents — if Agent A calls an MCP tool that Agent B doesn't have, the handoff fails
- **Error propagation** — agent failures cascade differently than human task failures
- **Observability** — AgentCat's new AX platform (announced yesterday) is starting to address this with session replay and goal detection

## Breakout: MCP Apps and The Agentic Web

**3:40 PM | Auditorium (Ground Floor)**

**Liad Yosef** (MCP Apps) presented on the emerging category of **MCP-powered applications** — apps designed to work with both humans and agents simultaneously.

The concept: instead of building separate interfaces for human users and AI agents, build one application that serves both through MCP. A developer tool might expose its functionality through the same MCP interface that both a CLI user and an agent can use.

## Breakout: From API Catalogs To Agent Catalogs

**3:40 PM | Emerald Room (1st Floor)**

**Vyshnavi Gadamsetti** and **Sebastian Wennemers** (SAP) presented on **agent catalog discovery** — solving the problem of finding the right MCP server among thousands of community-contributed options.

The session introduced **Open Resource Discovery** — a protocol for discovering, rating, and versioning MCP servers. This is critical infrastructure as the MCP ecosystem grows: with 89% of teams using MCP, the ability to find and evaluate the right tool server has become a first-order concern.

## Key Data Points from Day 2

The AAIF survey data was updated and displayed throughout Day 2:

- **MCP adoption**: 89% (up from pilot phase)
- **Multi-agent systems in production**: 60%
- **Open-source tooling**: 78% (vs 22% closed-source)
- **Stateless transport adoption**: 42% of MCP-using teams have migrated

## What This Means

Day 2 confirmed what Day 1 suggested: **the agentic stack is real**. The announcements weren't just product launches — they were infrastructure pieces clicking into place:

1. **MCP goes stateless** — Shaun Smith's keynote proved it scales
2. **A2A joins AAIF** — agent-to-agent communication now has a standards home
3. **Agent identity is solved** — for at least one vendor (Solo.io), with patterns others can follow
4. **Agent experience is emerging** — AgentCat's AX platform gives us tools to understand our agent users
5. **Governance is maturing** — Traefik's Sovereign Trust Plane, WSO2's Agent Manager, and Grape Up's Aiboostr provide concrete paths to production
