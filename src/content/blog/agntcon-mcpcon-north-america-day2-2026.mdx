*Friday morning in the keynote hall: MCP tool calls in Claude, 16x since April.*

Day two of AGNTCon + MCPCon North America, on Friday 23 October 2026, was a morning of keynotes and an afternoon of interviews: thirteen of them, from 09:37 to 16:36, recorded for [Udienza](/blog/udienza-video-podcast-launch-2026/). The day hour by hour is on udienza.com: [AGNTCon + MCPCon North America 2026: day two](https://www.udienza.com/articles/agntcon-mcpcon-north-america-2026-day-two). [My day one post](/blog/agntcon-mcpcon-north-america-2026/) covered MCP Tasks, Skills over MCP and elicitation.

Here is what I would pass on to a team that builds or runs agents.

## Smaller models, and a loop that never stops

Jonathan Bryce, Executive Director of Cloud & Infrastructure at the Linux Foundation, gave the PyTorch Foundation's keynote, "From Bigger Models to Better Systems". His argument: on the open LLM leaderboard, models under 13B parameters keep getting more capable at the same size, and small models frequently outperform larger ones.

The example that stayed with me came from Shopify. A small specialist model for one task (buyer profiles) runs in a loop in production: a calibrated judge detects the weak outputs, critics diagnose them and the model replays with their hints, and the accepted repairs become new training data. On the judge's score it went from 75.3 to 78.1 to 84.6 in a week, past the frontier model's 83.0.

![A slide titled A small specialist model can outperform a frontier model, the specialist's score rising from 75.3 to 84.6 above a frontier model line at 83.0](/blog/events/agntcon-mcpcon-north-america-day2-2026/agntcon-na-2026-day2-specialist-model.jpg)

The lesson for platform teams is not "use small models". It is that the evaluation loop (judge, repair, retrain) is the product, and the model is what it produces. If your agents have no judge in production, that is the piece to build first.

The tools are open, in a PyTorch Foundation that now has six projects: PyTorch, vLLM, DeepSpeed, Ray, Helion and Safetensors.

![A slide listing the six PyTorch Foundation projects: PyTorch, vLLM, DeepSpeed, Ray, Helion and Safetensors](/blog/events/agntcon-mcpcon-north-america-day2-2026/agntcon-na-2026-day2-pytorch-projects.jpg)

I sat down with Jonathan after his keynote for a 14-minute conversation on PyTorch, vLLM and the foundation, now in production for Udienza.

## Universal properties: guarantees for unpredictable agents

Shadaj Laddad, who leads research on Hydro at AWS, had the most engineering-minded talk of the conference. His answer to agents writing code you cannot fully predict is the **universal property**: a specification that applies to any application, whatever its logic. Rust's borrow checker enforces one (no concurrent access to mutable state), and so does React (an incremental render matches a render from scratch).

![The keynote hall from the back, the side screen reading universal properties: specifications that apply to any application, no matter the logic](/blog/events/agntcon-mcpcon-north-america-day2-2026/agntcon-na-2026-day2-universal-properties.jpg)

His two projects apply the idea: Hydro, a framework for correct, agent-written distributed systems, and Infinity, an agent runtime where concurrent agents behave the same as sequential execution. His advice for everyone else is the part to keep: find what is true of all your applications, define it independently of any one of them, and enforce it with frameworks, strong types and APIs. An agent can then write the logic, and the property still holds.

## The swap drill

Rao Surapaneni, VP of Gemini Enterprise & Agent Platform Governance at Google Cloud, asked the question worth putting to every team that runs agents: **can you run a swap drill in under two days?** Swap the model, across frontier, open-weight and proprietary. Swap the harness without rebuilding governance. Swap an agent, through A2A and a registry. And through every swap, identity, policy, observability and your business IP stay intact.

His six best practices read like a checklist for a platform team:

![A slide titled Best Practices with six numbered items for shipping autonomous agents safely](/blog/events/agntcon-mcpcon-north-america-day2-2026/agntcon-na-2026-day2-best-practices.jpg)

1. Demand standard interfaces in RFPs.
2. Emit OpenTelemetry GenAI traces.
3. Use federated, standards-based identity.
4. Store agent definitions as code.
5. Use an agent gateway to enforce enterprise policies.
6. Evals for everything.

If you run Kubernetes, most of this will sound familiar: declarative definitions in Git, a gateway for policy, standard telemetry. Agents are getting the same platform treatment, and the teams that already have it are ahead.

## MCP in Claude: 16x since April

Kelly Fang of Anthropic brought the day's biggest number: weekly MCP tool calls through Anthropic's remote MCP proxy grew **16x** since the MCP Dev Summit in New York in April 2026, with more than 6,000 servers in the Claude directory. One server reaches Claude on the web, desktop and mobile, Claude Code, Claude Managed Agents and Claude in Slack.

![A slide titled MCP is growing fast in Claude: 16x growth in weekly MCP tool calls since April 2026](/blog/events/agntcon-mcpcon-north-america-day2-2026/agntcon-na-2026-day2-mcp-16x.jpg)

Three builders put numbers on it from their side: Atlassian's MCP at more than 15 million daily tool calls, monday.com's Claude connector at 85,000 accounts, and Linear's issues created through MCP up 30x, more than all its other channels combined. For anyone still deciding whether to ship an MCP server for their product, that slide is the answer.

![A slide titled What we're hearing from builders, with quotes from Atlassian, monday.com and Linear](/blog/events/agntcon-mcpcon-north-america-day2-2026/agntcon-na-2026-day2-builders.jpg)

## On the floor: governance, six ways

The afternoon was interviews, about one every half hour. Listen to them side by side, as I did, and most of the vendors are selling a piece of the same thing: the controls around an agent in production. Each draws the line somewhere else.

- **Airia** puts the controls in a gateway: an MCP gateway and "agent constraints" that block what an agent is not meant to do, managed by IT in one place instead of a settings file on every laptop.
- **Alterion** watches from the network and the endpoint, where many agents have no control plane at all, and classifies their behaviour with its own AI.
- **Celigo** governs the MCP servers its integration platform publishes, down to single tools, and calls the path to autonomy "earned".
- **Diagrid** makes agents durable, so a crash does not send the same email twice, and signs every step an agent takes.
- **AgentCat** looks for the silent failures, when every call returns 200 and the user still had a bad experience.
- **Quesma** collects the agents' session logs, to see what agentic coding is worth and what it costs.

![Luca Berton and Spencer Reagan giving a thumbs-up at the Airia booth](/blog/events/agntcon-mcpcon-north-america-day2-2026/agntcon-na-2026-day2-airia.jpg)

Then there are the layers underneath: **Mezmo's** AURA, an open source SRE agent with a strict human in the loop before any remediation; **YugabyteDB's** Meko, a context layer of memory and knowledge for every agent; **Safe Software's** FME, which publishes no-code MCP servers over hard-to-reach data like 3D building models and sensor feeds.

![Andre Elizondo and Luca Berton in front of the Mezmo booth wall that reads Fortune tellers make bad SREs](/blog/events/agntcon-mcpcon-north-america-day2-2026/agntcon-na-2026-day2-mezmo.jpg)

**My take:** the questions to ask any of them are the same ones as in Amsterdam. Where does it run (on-premises, air-gapped, on Kubernetes)? Which identity standards does it speak? Does it emit standard telemetry? Can you leave without rewriting every agent? Those are Rao Surapaneni's swap drill, asked of a vendor.
