The Amsterdam Java User Group met tonight at **Metafactory** on the Zekeringstraat, 4th floor, with food and drinks from 18:00 and a plate of pita kebab in half the room. The first talk came from the host's own CTO: **Ricky van Rijn** with *"We Created Our Own AI Orchestrator And It Became So Much More!"*

![Wide shot of the meetup room at Metafactory: Ricky van Rijn stands behind a standing desk between two screens showing the OpenJCockpit logo, a pilot in a cockpit, with a boarding-pass banner on the left and the audience in the foreground.](/blog/events/amsterdam-jug-metafactory-openjcockpit-ai-orchestrator-2026/jug-metafactory-openjcockpit-room.jpg)

The "orchestrator" has a name: **OpenJCockpit**, "the open-source AI Software Factory."

## Who is speaking

Ricky's bio slide says he is **CTO at Metafactory**, and that he has "spent over a decade evolving from Java software engineer to CTO," working in environments "from agile startups to government institutions." That is a useful background for this topic: someone who has written the code, led the teams and sat through the procurement meetings now builds the tool that writes the code.

![Ricky van Rijn presenting his bio slide, 'CTO at MetaFactory', next to the OpenJCockpit banner.](/blog/events/amsterdam-jug-metafactory-openjcockpit-ai-orchestrator-2026/jug-metafactory-ricky-bio.jpg)

## You are the pilot, AI is your crew, quality is the destination

The whole talk is wrapped in an aviation metaphor, and the roll-up banner next to the screens shows it best. It is a **boarding pass**:

| Boarding pass field | Value |
|---|---|
| **From** | **YOU** (the pilot) |
| **To** | **QA** (quality) |
| **Pilot** | You |
| **Crew** | AI |
| **Destination** | Quality |

And at the bottom: *"You are the pilot. AI is your crew. Quality is the destination."* A QR code under "Scan to board" takes you to the project.

![Ricky van Rijn on stage between two screens with the OpenJCockpit pilot logo, in front of the audience.](/blog/events/amsterdam-jug-metafactory-openjcockpit-ai-orchestrator-2026/jug-metafactory-ricky-intro.jpg)

I like how honest this is. The route does not go from *you* to *code*. It goes from you to **QA**. The human does not disappear, and the human is not a typist either. The human is the one who is accountable for where the plane lands.

![Title slide 'We created our own AI orchestrator and it became so much more!' with the OpenJCockpit logo, shown while Ricky van Rijn gestures to the audience.](/blog/events/amsterdam-jug-metafactory-openjcockpit-ai-orchestrator-2026/jug-metafactory-title-slide.jpg)

## Three phases of building software

One slide, "Defining phases," puts the whole story in three columns:

| Phase | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Traditional** | Software engineers write code |
| **Assisted** | AI assists with writing |
| **Agentic** | AI writes code |

Most teams I meet are in the middle column: Copilot-style completion, chat in the IDE, a human in the driver's seat for every line. The jump to the third column is not a better model. It is a different way of working, and that is where an orchestrator comes in.

![Ricky van Rijn points at the 'Defining phases' slide: Traditional, Assisted, Agentic.](/blog/events/amsterdam-jug-metafactory-openjcockpit-ai-orchestrator-2026/jug-metafactory-defining-phases.jpg)

## Step by step towards agentic AI for client projects

The next slide is titled *"Step by step towards Agentic AI for client projects,"* with two ideas side by side:

- **The opportunity: AI drafts code.** An agent takes a task from a goal to working code on its own.
- **The multiplier: agents work in parallel.** Specialised agents handle different tasks at the same time, under a coordinating layer. That coordinating layer is the orchestrator.

The phrase *"step by step"* and the words *"client projects"* matter. This is not a demo toy. Metafactory builds software for clients, so the tool has to work under real constraints: reviews, quality gates and people who are accountable for the result.

![Ricky van Rijn presents the slide 'Step by step towards Agentic AI for client projects', with 'AI drafts code' and 'Agents work in parallel' illustrations.](/blog/events/amsterdam-jug-metafactory-openjcockpit-ai-orchestrator-2026/jug-metafactory-agentic-step-by-step.jpg)

## What the abstract adds

The meetup abstract says the team built the orchestrator with **Embabel Agent**, **Skills** and **Spec files**, with a **visual dashboard** for active agents, tasks and next steps. And it describes how roles change: developers review AI output, analysts define requirements, and product owners manage faster sprint queues.

Read together with the slides, the picture is consistent: **specs go in, agents work in parallel, a dashboard shows what is happening, and humans own quality at the end.** (Embabel is the JVM agent framework with goal-oriented planning, a natural choice for a Java user group.)

## My takeaway

Platform and engineering leaders ask me every week: *"Will AI replace our developers?"* The OpenJCockpit boarding pass is a better answer than any slide of mine: **the destination is quality, and somebody has to be the pilot.**

If you want the other half of the agentic story, the next talk of the night covered how agents *find and call* capabilities: Capability Mesh. The last one, Securing Java for the Quantum Era, was about the part of the stack nobody wants to think about until it is too late.
