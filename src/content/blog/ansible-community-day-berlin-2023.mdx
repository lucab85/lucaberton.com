## Berlin, Germany – September 20, 2023

Ansible Community Day Berlin 2023 brought together developers, sysadmins, and automation engineers from across Europe. Hosted by Red Hat and powered by the Ansible community, the event focused on collaboration, open innovation, and sharing real-world use cases.

The venue was **c-base** in Berlin.

![Luca Berton taking a selfie in the packed main hall at c-base during Ansible Community Day Berlin 2023](/blog/events/ansible-community-day-berlin-2023/luca-c-base-hall.jpg)

*The main hall at c-base between sessions, with the audience settling in.*

## Talks I watched

### Automation Good Practices and the Zen of Ansible

The morning good-practices session covered dynamic inventory: the screen showed an example project with `dynamic_inventory_plugin.yml`, `dynamic_inventory_script.py` and per-group files. A later slide read "And don't forget the Zen of ~~Python~~ Ansible", credited "By Guru Tim Appnel".

![A slide at c-base reading "And don't forget the Zen of Python Ansible, by Guru Tim Appnel", with the two presenters on stage](/blog/events/ansible-community-day-berlin-2023/zen-of-ansible-slide.jpg)

*The Zen of Ansible slide during the Automation Good Practices session.*

### Steampunk Spotter: Ansible scanning tools

The XLAB Steampunk session included an overview slide titled "Ansible scanning tools": Ansible Lint, Ansible Later, Ansible Navigator, Ansible Molecule, KICS (Keeping Infrastructure as Code Secure), Deepsource and Steampunk Spotter. Then came "Selecting and scanning projects: Ansible Lockdown, RedHat COP, etc.", with the `ansible-lockdown/RHEL7-CIS` repository on screen. In the part I recorded, the speaker walked through the resulting pull requests: one where fully qualified collection names were fixed, and one with best-practice changes, where Spotter's `--rewrite` option rewrote a task to use the extended syntax.

![The "Ansible scanning tools" slide from the Steampunk session at c-base, listing Ansible Lint, Ansible Later, Ansible Navigator, Ansible Molecule, KICS, Deepsource and Steampunk Spotter](/blog/events/ansible-community-day-berlin-2023/steampunk-scanning-tools.jpg)

*The Steampunk Spotter session's map of Ansible scanning tools.*

My take: running a scanner across well-known public roles is a good way to calibrate it before you point it at your own estate. You find out quickly which findings are noise and which are real.

### Arista AVD in a devcontainer

The Arista session started with "What is Ansible AVD?". AVD stands for Arista Validated Design, as it was based on the EVPN Deployment Guide. The slide called it a community project for deploying EVPN-based data centre fabrics, with over 200 stars on GitHub and 79 contributors as of September 2023, and the most active Arista collection on Ansible Galaxy. The high-level workflow was:

1. Define abstracted group and host vars using the AVD data model.
2. Generate low-level, device-specific variables (structured configs).
3. Parse templates and build plain-text configs.
4. Deliver configs to network devices using `arista.eos.eos_config`.

![The "What is Ansible AVD?" slide at c-base, with the AVD workflow diagram and the presenter at the lectern](/blog/events/ansible-community-day-berlin-2023/arista-avd-slide.jpg)

*Arista Validated Design explained during the devcontainer session.*
