When Apple takes 30% of every dollar your app makes, you need every edge. The good news? Most first-time app builders leave **15–30% of their potential revenue on the table** not because of bad code or poor marketing, but because they never touch four levers that Apple itself provides.

Here's how to keep more of what you earn — backed by data from RevenueCat and Adapty's 2026 reports covering 115,000+ apps and $16 billion in revenue.

## 1. Apple's Small Business Program — Instant 15% Back

**The rule:** If you earned $1 million or less in App Store proceeds in the previous calendar year, you qualify for Apple's [App Store Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/). Instead of 30% going to Apple, only 15% is deducted. For every $1 your app makes, you keep **$0.85 instead of $0.70**.

| Status | Apple Takes | You Keep | On a $10 sale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard rate | 30% | 70% | $7.00 |
| Small Business Program | 15% | 85% | $8.50 |
| Subscriptions after year 1 | 15% | 85% | $8.50 |

**On $500K annual revenue, that's $75,000 back in your pocket.**

### How to enroll (10 minutes)

1. Go to the [enrollment page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/enroll/) and sign in as the Account Holder
2. Review your associated developer accounts (Apple combines proceeds across all accounts you control)
3. Accept the latest Paid Applications agreement in App Store Connect
4. Accept the Small Business Program terms

Your 15% rate kicks in ~15 days after approval (tied to Apple's fiscal month schedule). If you fall back under $1M in a future year, you re-qualify automatically.

> **Pro tip:** If you use RevenueCat or Adapty, update your enrollment status in their dashboards. RevenueCat requires you to set your entry date per-app under *Apps & Providers*. Adapty uses your membership periods to calculate correct proceeds in charts. Without this, your analytics will overstate Apple's cut.

## 2. Billing Grace Period — Recover 15-20% of Churned Revenue

When a subscriber's card fails on renewal, Apple doesn't immediately cancel them. With **Billing Grace Period** enabled, they keep full access to your app for up to 28 days while Apple silently retries the charge. If the retry succeeds, you keep the subscriber with zero churn — zero revenue loss.

Apps with grace period enabled recover **15–20% more subscriptions** than those without it, according to Adapty's data. Involuntary churn from expired or declined cards accounts for up to 20% of all subscription payment attempts — this is the single biggest silent revenue leak.

### How it works

| Grace period | Access continues | Apple retries |
|---|---|---|
| **3 days** | 3 days | Few retries |
| **16 days** | 16 days | Standard retries |
| **28 days** | 28 days | Maximum retries |

After the grace period, if Apple still can't collect, the subscription enters the standard **billing retry state** (up to 60 days of additional retries). But the grace period is your first line of defense — it keeps the user happy and in the app while giving payment recovery the best chance.

### Enable it

1. In App Store Connect → **Settings** → scroll to *Paid Applications*
2. Click **Manage** next to Billing Grace Period
3. Select duration (3, 16, or 28 days) per subscription group

> **Important:** For weekly subscriptions, Apple caps the grace period even if you select 28 days — the effective window is shorter to prevent abuse. Choose 28 days for monthly+ subscriptions.

## 3. Trial Length — The Day-0 Conversion Cliff

This is where most first-time app builders make their single biggest mistake: picking a trial length based on gut feeling instead of data.

RevenueCat's 2026 State of Subscription Apps report (115,000 apps, $16B revenue) shows the conversion math clearly:

| Trial Duration | Median Conversion | Key Insight |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 days | 30% | Lowest conversion — users feel rushed |
| 5–9 days | 45% | Sweet spot — 52% of apps use this |
| 10–16 days | 44% | Balanced conversion vs. churn |
| 17–32 days | 45.7% | Highest conversion, but 51% cancel before end |

But here's the nuance the data reveals: **70% better conversion with longer trials, but apps keep moving to 3-day**. Why? AI-driven variable costs and shorter payback periods make immediate revenue look attractive. It's the wrong trade-off.

### The real numbers

Out of 100 users who start a trial:

| Trial Type | Convert to Paying | Stay After Cancel Deal |
|---|---|---|
| 3-day trial | 25 | ~2 |
| 2-week trial | 42 | ~8 |

Half pick the short one. But that short trial converts at 25% vs 42% for two weeks. The 17-point gap means **+17 more paying users** out of every 100 who start a trial.

### Where the paywall goes matters most

RevenueCat data shows **over 80% of trial starts happen on Day 0** — the moment of download. If your paywall doesn't convince the user in their first session, you may lose them entirely. Show the paywall **at the start**, right after onboarding, with the free trial active. Don't bury it.

## 4. The Cancel Page Deal — 4x Retention

When a user hits "cancel subscription," most apps show a generic confirmation screen. That's leaving money on the table.

Apps that show a **cancel-page deal** — a discount offer or extended access — see **4x more users stay** compared to a plain cancel flow.

The math from Adapty: **8 of 100 users** who see a deal offer stay, versus **2 of 100** who cancel through a standard flow. That's a 4x improvement in retention from a single screen.

### Best practices

- **Show the offer immediately** when the cancel flow starts — don't make them dig
- **Use urgency**: "50% off for 3 months if you stay now"
- **Track the source**: Segment which acquisition channel produces users who respond to cancel offers
- **Automate**: Use RevenueCat's [Customer Center](https://www.revenuecat.com/feature/customer-center) or Adapty's built-in retention flows

## 5. The Vibe-Coded App Invoice — What Skipping Compliance Really Costs

The first four levers are about keeping more of the revenue you already earn. This one is about not losing all of it at once.

"Vibe-coded" apps — shipped fast from AI-generated boilerplate — routinely skip basic legal guardrails that have nothing to do with code quality. Regulators don't care that a chatbot wrote your signup flow; they care whether it complies with the law. Here's the invoice a small app can rack up when a handful of shortcuts get caught at the same time:

| Violation | What Happened | Statutory Exposure | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **COPPA** — no age gate | Collected data from under-13 users without parental consent | Up to $53,000 per child | × 2,410 users |
| **CIPA** — session replay | Recorded user sessions (FullStory/Hotjar-style tools) without consent | ~$5,000 per session | × 380 sessions |
| **CAN-SPAM** — no unsubscribe link | Sent marketing email with no working opt-out | Up to $53,000 per email | × 1,204 emails |
| **Google Fonts** — IP leak | Loaded fonts from Google's CDN, leaking visitor IPs without consent (GDPR) | ~€100 per visitor | Per EU visitor |
| **DMCA** — no registered agent | No designated agent on file with the Copyright Office, forfeiting safe harbor | Up to $150,000 per work (willful) | Per infringing upload |
| **Silent renewal** — no disclosure | Auto-renewed subscriptions without clear disclosure (FTC "click-to-cancel" rule) | Full refund of renewal revenue | Per subscriber |

Stack even a few of these against a few thousand users and a scrappy app can face a compliance bill in the tens of millions — all from gaps that cost nothing to close before launch.

### Close each gap in an afternoon

- **COPPA:** Add an age gate. If you knowingly serve under-13 users, obtain verifiable parental consent before collecting any data.
- **CIPA / session replay:** Disclose session recording in your privacy policy and get affirmative consent where wiretapping-style laws apply.
- **CAN-SPAM:** Every marketing email needs a working unsubscribe link, honored within 10 business days.
- **Google Fonts:** Self-host font files instead of loading them from Google's CDN (or use a bundler like `next/font`) so visitor IPs never leave your server.
- **DMCA:** Register your agent with the [US Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/) — a one-time $6 fee that preserves safe harbor.
- **Silent renewals:** Disclose auto-renewal terms clearly at purchase and make cancellation as easy as signup, per the FTC's 2026 "click to cancel" rule.

None of these take more than an afternoon to fix. Every one of them is cheaper than the first violation.

## 6. The 5 Security Mistakes Every Vibe-Coded App Makes

Compliance gaps cost you in fines. These cost you the app itself — an account-takeover incident or a leaked admin panel ends the business faster than any regulator does. AI coding assistants are excellent at shipping a working feature and consistently silent about the security control that should ship with it. The same five gaps show up in almost every fast-shipped app:

| Mistake | Why It Happens | What an Attacker Does |
|---|---|---|
| **Session token in `localStorage`** | Easiest place for AI-generated auth code to read/write a token | Any XSS on the page reads the token and hijacks the session |
| **Client-side admin checks** | `if (user.isAdmin)` in the frontend feels like it "works" in a demo | Attacker edits the JS/state in devtools and unlocks admin UI with no server check |
| **No 2FA or OTP** | Adds a step to the signup flow the AI wasn't asked to build | Leaked or guessed passwords convert directly into full account takeover |
| **`/login` has no rate limiting** | Rate limiting is infra, not a "feature," so it's rarely prompted for | Unlimited password-spraying and credential-stuffing against every account |
| **No password strength check** | The form "works" with any string, so it looks done | Weak/reused passwords make the two mistakes above trivial to exploit at scale |

The pattern behind all five: each one makes a *demo* work perfectly while leaving the *production* path wide open. Vibe-coding optimizes for "the feature works when I click it," not "the feature survives someone who wants to break it."

### Close each gap before you have real users

- **Session tokens:** Store them in an `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite` cookie, never in `localStorage` or `sessionStorage` — those are readable by any script that runs on your page.
- **Admin checks:** Every privileged action must be re-verified server-side against the authenticated session. A client-side `isAdmin` flag is a UI convenience, never a security boundary.
- **2FA/OTP:** Add TOTP (Google Authenticator-style) or email/SMS OTP for account-level actions — sign-in, password reset, billing changes. Most auth providers (Supabase Auth, Clerk, Auth0) enable this with a few lines of config.
- **Rate limiting on `/login`:** Cap attempts per IP and per account (e.g., 5 attempts / 15 minutes) and back off exponentially. A reverse proxy rule or middleware is enough — you don't need a WAF on day one.
- **Password strength:** Enforce a minimum length (12+) and check against a breached-password list (e.g., the [Have I Been Pwned Passwords API](https://haveibeenpwned.com/API/v3#PwnedPasswords)) at signup and reset.

None of these require a security team. They require asking your AI coding assistant for them explicitly — it will build them if you ask, but it will not volunteer them.

## The Bottom Line: Add 15–30% to Your First Dollar (And Don't Give It Back)

| Lever | Revenue Impact | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Small Business Program | +15% commission | 10 minutes |
| Billing Grace Period | +15-20% fewer churn losses | 2 minutes in App Store Connect |
| Optimize Trial Length | +17pp conversion | A/B test, 1 week |

These aren't advanced growth hacks — they're baseline defaults that Apple, RevenueCat, Adapty, and federal/state regulators all expect you to use. The only question is whether you'll use them before or after they cost you.

**Checklist for day one:**
1. ✅ Enroll in the [Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/enroll/)
2. ✅ Enable Billing Grace Period (28 days) in App Store Connect
3. ✅ Set your free trial to 7–14 days (not 3)
4. ✅ Show the paywall on Day 0, not after the user "explores"
5. ✅ Deploy a cancel-page deal flow before your first user cancels
6. ✅ Add an age gate, unsubscribe links, a DMCA agent, and self-hosted fonts before you have your first 100 users
