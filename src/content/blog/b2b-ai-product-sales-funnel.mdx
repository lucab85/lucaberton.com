## The Funnel That Doesn't Break for AI

I don't normally look at Instagram for sales frameworks — but [saasyass](https://www.instagram.com/saasyass/) posted a 6-step funnel that cuts through the noise. It's clean: Traffic → Landing Page → Qualify → Book → Confirm → Sales, paired with three ad angles (Words, Problems, Objections) and a tracking chain that ends at a single checkpoint most funnels skip: *Actually Signed*.

What makes this framework worth adapting isn't its novelty. It's how cleanly the gaps show up when you try to apply it to AI products. In B2B software, "Book" means a calendar invite. In AI, "Book" means convincing a team to let you touch their data for a proof of concept. In B2B software, "Confirm" means a verbal yes. In AI, "Confirm" means clearing three stakeholders who each have different risk tolerances. In B2B software, "Sales" means a contract. In AI, "Sales" means a contract *and* a data processing agreement *and* a model performance SLA.

The funnel doesn't break for AI — it reveals where AI sales actually live. Here's how to run it.

## The 6-Step Funnel, Mapped to AI Products

### 1. Traffic — Where AI buyers start searching

In traditional B2B, traffic comes from search ads, LinkedIn, and event booths. In AI, buyers search differently:

- **Benchmark queries**: "Claude 3.5 vs GPT-4o code editing speed"
- **Technical docs**: "How to fine-tune Llama 3.2 on Kubernetes"
- **Stack-specific searches**: "LangChain alternative for production"
- **Integration lookups**: "OpenAI-compatible API providers"

**Practical shift:** Your top-of-funnel content needs to answer technical questions, not pitch features. Blog posts with real benchmarks, deployment guides, and integration walkthroughs pull in qualified AI buyers who are already comparing options.

**Example:** A team selling an LLM evaluation platform writes "Evaluating Llama 3.2 on RKE2 vs. EKS vs. Lambda" — the traffic is engineers who are 3 weeks into their AI project and already have budget approval.

### 2. Landing Page — Where AI buyers evaluate

Traditional landing pages optimize for form fills. AI landing pages need to answer: "Can this actually work with my stack?"

Key elements for AI product landing pages:

| Element | Traditional SaaS | AI Product |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing | Tiered plans | Cost per token, GPU hours, model serving |
| Proof | Testimonials | Benchmarks, latency graphs, accuracy metrics |
| Integration | API keys | Data pipelines, model registries, MLOps tools |
| Security | SOC 2 | Data handling, model IP, privacy controls |

**Practical shift:** Lead with technical proof, not business benefits. Put a benchmark comparison above the fold. Link to a Colab notebook they can run themselves. Show a deployment guide for their exact stack (e.g., "Deploy on EKS with Terraform").

### 3. Qualify — Filtering for AI-ready buyers

Not every lead is ready for an AI product. The qualification questions diverge sharply:

**Technical readiness:**
- What's their MLOps maturity? (No MLOps = 6-month delay, not 6-week sale)
- Can they get data access approval? (Data is the #1 blocker in AI deployments)
- Do they have GPU budget? (Cloud GPU costs are 3-5x equivalent CPU/SaaS costs)

**Use case fit:**
- Are they solving a problem where AI actually helps, or where a rule-based system would suffice?
- Is their data structured enough for the model, or will they need data engineering first?

**Procurement reality:**
- How long is their vendor approval cycle? (AI purchases often need security + legal sign-off)
- Do they have a dedicated AI budget, or are they cannibalizing headcount?

**Practical shift:** Add a technical discovery call before the demo. Spend 30 minutes understanding their stack and constraints, then customize the demo with their actual data (or a close proxy).

### 4. Book — Scheduling the AI evaluation

In traditional B2B, booking means a 30-minute demo. In AI, booking means aligning on a proof-of-concept.

**What changes:**
- You need at least 2 stakeholders on the call (technical + business)
- The agenda shifts from "show features" to "scope the POC"
- You need to agree on success criteria before the demo ends

**Sample agenda for an AI product booking call:**
1. **10 min** — Confirm their current process and pain points
2. **10 min** — Show a relevant benchmark or result (not a feature tour)
3. **10 min** — Scope the POC: data access, timeline, success metrics
4. **5 min** — Next steps and timeline

**Practical shift:** Come prepared with a POC framework, not a slide deck. Bring a one-pager that says "Here's what we'd test, here's the data we'd need, here's how long it takes."

### 5. Confirm — Locking in the AI evaluation

This is where most AI deals stall. The prospect said "yes" to a POC, but now they need to get data approval, budget approval, and stakeholder alignment internally.

**The 3 confirmations you need:**
1. **Technical confirmation** — The demo/POC results met their expectations
2. **Business confirmation** — The ROI justifies the cost
3. **Procurement confirmation** — The legal/security process is initiated

**Practical shift:** Send a "POC Results Report" — not a sales deck. Include actual numbers from their data, a clear "before/after" comparison, and a 3-sentence recommendation. The report should be shareable internally without your sales rep on the call.

### 6. Sales — Closing the AI deal

The final hurdle in AI sales isn't price — it's risk allocation.

**Common final objections and how to address them:**

| Objection | Why it's real | How to address it |
|---|---|---|
| "We need model transparency" | Regulatory + trust | Provide inference logs, model cards, audit trails |
| "Data privacy concerns" | Legal + compliance | On-prem/hybrid deployment, data deletion guarantees |
| "Integration is too complex" | Engineering bandwidth | Pre-built connectors, Terraform modules, step-by-step guides |
| "ROI is unclear" | Finance scrutiny | Pilot pricing, success-based pricing, clear metrics |

**The contract structure for AI:**
1. Master Service Agreement (MSA)
2. Data Processing Agreement (DPA)
3. Model Performance SLA (separate from uptime SLA)
4. Implementation plan with milestones

**Practical shift:** Price the first year as a POC + production bundle. Most AI deals collapse because the POC succeeds but the enterprise sale stalls on contract terms. Bundle them so the same negotiation covers both.

## The Three Ad Angles, Adapted for AI

[saasyass](https://www.instagram.com/saasyass/)'s three angles are hooks that map to stages of the funnel. Here's how they translate for AI products:

### WORDS — From "Stop Chasing Leads" to "Stop Chasing POCs"

The original hook is "NO MORE CHASING LEADS" → headline "STOP CHASING LEADS."

**AI application:** AI teams spend 80% of their time chasing experiments that never ship. The hook isn't "our platform" — it's "stop chasing model drift you never catch."

**Example angles:**
- "Stop chasing false positives in your production alerts"
- "Stop chasing model accuracy you can't reproduce"
- "Stop chasing GPU bills from unoptimized deployments"

The pattern: identify a *repetitive, frustrating task* that AI product teams do today, and position your product as the end of that chase.

### PROBLEM — From "Leads Went Cold" to "POCs Go Dark"

The original hook is "OUR LEADS WENT COLD" → headline "LEADS GOING COLD?"

**AI application:** AI POCs go dark because of *evaluation complexity*. The lead didn't reject your product — they got buried in the internal process of getting data approved, model validated, and stakeholders aligned.

**Hook angles:**
- "Are your POCs going dark after week 2?"
- "Is your evaluation stuck on data access?"
- "Did your proof-of-concept die in legal review?"

The AI-specific problem isn't that leads go cold — it's that *evaluations* go cold. Address that explicitly in your follow-up sequence.

### OBJECTION — "Setup Looks Hard" (It Actually Is)

The original hook is "SETUP LOOKS HARD" → headline "WORRIED ABOUT SETUP?"

**AI application:** This isn't just perceived — AI setup *is* genuinely hard. Data pipelines, model serving, integration with existing systems, GPU provisioning — these are real engineering tasks, not marketing copy.

**Hook angles:**
- "Worried about model deployment?"
- "Worried about your data pipeline?"
- "Worried about GPU costs?"

But unlike traditional SaaS (where you can downplay the setup), the key is to *acknowledge* the complexity and then show how you've reduced it. "Yes, deploying LLMs is hard. Here's how we made it a 3-command operation."

## The Call Sequence: CALL 1 → CALL 2 → CALL 3

The Reel shows three call stages. In AI sales, these map to:

### CALL 1 — Problem Identification

**Goal:** Uncover the AI readiness gap

**Questions to ask:**
- "What's your current MLOps stack, and what breaks most often?"
- "When was the last time a model went to production, and how long did it take?"
- "What's the #1 bottleneck in your AI workflow right now?"

**Success signal:** They name a specific, measurable pain point (not "we need AI")

### CALL 2 — Solution Presentation

**Goal:** Show a relevant result with their context

**Format:**
- 5 min: Recap their pain point (proves you listened)
- 10 min: Show a benchmark/result (not a feature tour)
- 5 min: Map their stack to your solution

**Key rule:** If you can't tailor this call to their specific use case, reschedule it. Generic AI demos are worse than no demo.

### CALL 3 — Commitment

**Goal:** Lock in the POC scope and timeline

**What to present:**
- A one-page POC plan with dates and success criteria
- A cost estimate (transparency beats surprise)
- A list of what they need to prepare (data, access, stakeholders)

**Success signal:** They leave with a written scope, not verbal agreement

## Tracking Metrics: Tracked → Qualified → Closed → Revenue

The framework's tracking chain is: Tracked → Qualified → Closed → Revenue → *Actually Signed*. Here's how to operationalize each for AI products:

### Tracked — Who found you through technical content

In AI, tracking starts upstream of your funnel:

| Source | What "tracked" means |
|---|---|
| Blog/SEO | Engineers reading technical content |
| GitHub stars/forks | Developers testing your code |
| Hugging Face downloads | ML engineers evaluating your models |
| Conference talks | Decision-makers hearing your perspective |

**Key metric:** Of tracked visitors, how many engage with technical content (benchmark reports, deployment guides) vs. marketing pages? The former predicts downstream conversion.

### Qualified — Technical + business readiness

Not all qualified leads are equal in AI:

**Score them on two axes:**
- **Technical readiness** (0-10): MLOps maturity, data access, GPU budget
- **Business urgency** (0-10): Timeline, budget approval, stakeholder alignment

A lead with 8/10 technical and 3/10 business will stall. A lead with 5/10 technical and 8/10 business will over-promise. Look for 6+/6+ — the sweet spot where both sides are ready.

### Closed — POCs completed with success criteria met

This is the critical bottleneck. Most AI sales funnels track "POC started" — the framework tracks "POC closed."

**Three closure criteria:**
1. **Technical success:** The model meets the agreed accuracy/latency targets
2. **Integration success:** It works with their existing stack
3. **Adoption success:** At least 2 team members can use it independently

If only criterion #1 is met, you've built a demo, not a sale.

### Revenue → ACTUALLY SIGNED

The gap between "contract signed" and "payment received" is wider in AI than in traditional B2B:

- **Contract signing** → legal + procurement (4-12 weeks)
- **First payment** → implementation milestone #1 (30-60 days after kickoff)
- **Full payment** → production deployment (90-180 days after contract)

**Track at two points:**
1. Contract signed (commitment)
2. First payment received (actual revenue)

The framework's "Actually Signed" checkpoint means: don't count a deal until money hits the bank, not until the contract is signed.

## The Production AI Connection

This funnel mirrors the Production AI Framework™ — the progression from Prototype to Platform to Production to Proof. They align because the sales funnel maps to the *buyer's* AI maturity journey:

- **Traffic → Prototype**: They're exploring AI options, testing feasibility
- **Landing Page → Platform**: They're assessing whether a platform approach makes sense
- **Qualify → Production**: They're evaluating production readiness (data, compliance, scaling)
- **Book → Proof**: They're ready to prove ROI with real data
- **Confirm → Proof**: They've proven ROI internally and gotten approval
- **Sales → Proof**: They've committed to production deployment

When your funnel stage matches their production stage, conversion rates increase 3-5x. A "Prototype-stage" buyer (week 1-2) needs a different touch than a "Proof-stage" buyer (week 3-4 on your platform).

**Practical application:** Segment your pipeline by production stage, not just deal size. Build your nurture sequence around the buyer's progression from Prototype to Proof, and time your asks accordingly.

## What to Watch

- **The "Book" stage is the conversion cliff.** If your meeting-to-POC conversion is under 40%, your demos aren't tailored enough. Rescope.
- **Track "POC closed," not "POC started."** Most funnels stop at "interested" — this one doesn't. Build your dashboard around "Actually Signed."
- **Adapt the objection hook to AI's real complexity.** "Setup looks hard" is true for AI. Acknowledge it, then reduce it.
- **Map the funnel to your buyer's production stage.** Match your messaging to their AI maturity, not your product features.
- **Bundle POC + production contracts.** The gap between "POC success" and "enterprise contract" kills more AI deals than price.
