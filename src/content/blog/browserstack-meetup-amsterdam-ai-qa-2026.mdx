Tonight I am heading to the **BrowserStack Meetup Group — Amsterdam**, an evening dedicated to one of the most consequential shifts in our field: how artificial intelligence is reshaping quality assurance. The meetup runs from 6:00 PM to 8:00 PM CEST at Capital C Diamantbeurs, right in the heart of Amsterdam.

The world of QA is changing fast. AI promises huge efficiency gains in test automation, but it also introduces new failure modes — model hallucinations, agents drowning in excessive context, brittle tests that look green but verify nothing. Tonight's agenda is built around exactly that tension: how to get the leverage of AI without the pitfalls.

## The Agenda

The evening is structured around two complementary talks, with plenty of room for networking:

- **6:00 PM** — Welcome and Networking
- **6:10 PM** — *Just Enough Context: Teaching Claude to Test with Playwright* — Eleonora Belova
- **6:50 PM** — Break
- **7:00 PM** — *Generative AI in Quality Engineering: Beyond the Hype* — Robert Jadoenandansing
- **7:40 PM** — Dinner and Networking

## Talk 1: Just Enough Context — Teaching Claude to Test with Playwright

**Eleonora Belova** (QA Automation Engineer at Virtual Vaults) tackles one of the subtlest problems in AI-assisted testing: context. Give Claude too little and the tests are shallow; give it too much and it hallucinates or loses the thread. Her talk focuses on precisely controlling the context you feed an LLM so it produces reliable, hallucination-free Playwright tests.

This is squarely in the territory of **context engineering** — the discipline of deciding what an agent can see, and what it should ignore. For anyone building AI-driven test automation, the lesson is that the model is only as good as the window you give it.

## Talk 2: Generative AI in Quality Engineering — Beyond the Hype

**Robert Jadoenandansing** (Director at TestBotics Academy) takes a wider lens, looking at the real-world impact of Generative AI across the entire QA lifecycle — from test design through execution to debugging. The "beyond the hype" framing matters: the goal is actionable knowledge about where GenAI genuinely helps and where the limitations, risks, and ethical considerations bite, especially in enterprise QA environments.

## Why This Matters

Both talks converge on a theme I keep returning to: AI does not replace the QA engineer's judgment, it changes the shape of the work. The new skills are about scoping models, validating their output, and knowing when a generated test is trustworthy. That is as much a prompt-engineering and platform-engineering problem as a pure testing one.

If you work in QA, test automation, or AI-assisted development and you are in Amsterdam tonight, this is exactly the kind of focused community evening worth showing up for.
