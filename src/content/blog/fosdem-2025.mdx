## More conversations from the floor

Not every conversation made the ranked list. Here are three more I recorded between the devrooms and the stands.

### Peter (CODE University of Applied Sciences): satellite data for Tempelhofer Feld

On Saturday afternoon I met Peter, a student at CODE University of Applied Sciences in Berlin. Last semester his group built an open-source project that combines satellite data with weather data to observe the microclimate of Tempelhofer Feld, the former airport in the middle of Berlin. The website that displays the data is built with Vue, and the back end is written in Python. Weather data comes from Open-Meteo and satellite data currently comes from Google Earth Engine. Peter explained that the team caches much of the data themselves and kept the data sources separate enough that they can switch to another satellite provider if they find a suitable one.

The goal was to see what vegetation indices can tell you about plant health in a defined area: how green the grass on the field is through the year. Their finding, as Peter put it, is that Berlin winters are probably getting milder, so the field stays greener for more of the year, even though the summers are drier.

### Christoph (NetBSD): portability and build.sh

On Sunday morning of FOSDEM's 25th edition I spotted Christoph by his NetBSD shirt. He has been a NetBSD developer since the early 1990s. He described NetBSD as an operating system and a full distribution, very portable, with a strong emphasis on security and on keeping things simple. His example was how easy it is to build: check out the source tree and run one script, `build.sh`, which cross-compiles the whole system and produces bootable media. According to Christoph, the result runs on everything from the smallest computers to large ones.

When I asked what was new this year, he said the project had done so much that he hadn't kept track, and reminded me it was Sunday morning after a day of networking events.

My take: `build.sh` is the pattern I want from platform tooling. One entry point builds its own toolchain and gives you the same result whether you target your own machine or a different architecture.

### Raine Mäkeläinen (Jolla): Sailfish OS 5.0, the Jolla C2 and the Jolla Mind2

![The Jolla x Reeder C2 Community Edition phone held up at the Linux on Mobile stand at FOSDEM 2025](/blog/events/fosdem-2025/fosdem-2025-jolla-c2-community-phone.jpg)

*The Jolla C2 Community Edition at the Linux on Mobile stand.*

On Sunday afternoon I visited the Linux on Mobile stand, where Raine Mäkeläinen, Chief Engineer at Jolla, showed me the Jolla C2 Community Phone. It runs Sailfish OS 5.0, and Raine said Jolla had released 5.0 for all Sailfish-supported devices, including several Sony Xperia models, on the Thursday of that week. According to Raine, the release also improved microG support. With Sailfish's Android app support plus microG, many Android apps work, including banking and mobile payment apps. Some still don't: Netflix, with its DRM, is a hard one to crack. Sailfish is open source, but the Android app support is a different matter.

When I asked about the shiny box on the table, he introduced the Jolla Mind2, "your personal AI computer": a box that runs AI models locally, with no cloud, as an assistant for your email and for documents you upload and can then query.

My take: Mind2 applies on a small scale what I recommend to enterprises: keep the data where it lives and bring the model to it.

More on Jolla's products at [jolla.com](https://jolla.com).
