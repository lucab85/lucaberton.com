The IBC2026 Innovation Awards recognised collaborative projects driving innovation across the media, entertainment, and technology industries. More than 120 deployed innovation projects were considered by the 2026 judging panel.

![IBC 2026 exhibition hall during the awards season](/blog/events/ibc-2026/day2-morning-hall.jpg)

*The exhibition hall during the final days of IBC 2026.*

## Award categories and winners

Winners were revealed across four categories: **Content Creation**, **Content Distribution**, **Content Everywhere**, and **Social Impact**. This year's finalists reflected the growing impact of AI, cloud-native and software-defined workflows, open standards, new approaches to live production, and greater accessibility.

### Content Creation finalists

**The Collective Newsroom** powering BBC India builds a unified multilingual production operation. The project brings together diverse linguistic and cultural teams under a single, collaborative production environment, enabling seamless content creation across India's complex media landscape.

**The Media eXchange Layer (MXL)** — an open-source initiative from the EBU and NABA supported by the Linux Foundation. MXL establishes an open, interoperable fabric for software-defined live production, enabling media organisations to build flexible, vendor-agnostic production infrastructures.

**The Formula E Strategy Agent** uses AI to process live racing data and simplify complex strategies for viewers. By transforming raw telemetry and timing data into intuitive visual narratives, the system helps audiences understand the strategic nuances of electric motorsport in real time.

### Content Distribution finalists

**Reuters' "Capacity that follows the live news story"** uses TVU MediaMesh to create cloud-native, event-driven news distribution. The system dynamically allocates bandwidth and routing based on breaking news events, ensuring that field reporters always have the capacity they need when they need it.

**TCODE (Technical Content Delivery)** is a centralised digital contribution and workflow orchestration platform developed with Ideal Systems for Radio Televisyen Malaysia. The system streamlines the ingest, processing, and distribution of broadcast content across multiple platforms and formats.

**RTBF Belgium's move to standards-based server-guided ad insertion**, launched for the FIFA World Cup with Bitmovin's Player and AIP's AdBlendr. The implementation enables personalised advertising in live streaming while maintaining broadcast-grade quality and reliability.

### Content Everywhere finalists

**Globo's DTV+ implementation** in Brazil demonstrates the power of digital terrestrial television enhanced with interactive services. The system brings advanced features like personalised recommendations, interactive voting, and on-demand content to traditional broadcast television.

**The Premier League Companion** powered by Copilot offers fans an enhanced viewing experience with real-time statistics, tactical analysis, and multi-angle replays integrated directly into the broadcast.

**AI-generated performer Tilly Norwood** — a synthetic actor created using AI-assisted animation and voice synthesis — demonstrates how virtual performers can be integrated into live and on-demand content production.

### Social Impact finalisers

**ITU's AI for Good** initiative applies artificial intelligence to address global challenges in healthcare, education, and environmental sustainability. The programme connects AI researchers with social impact organisations to develop practical solutions.

**The IOC Cyber Abuse Protection Service** protects participants at the Olympic Games from online abuse, using a combination of AI detection and human moderation to identify and respond to harassment in real time.

**Closing the Sign Language Gap** — an AI-powered solution developed by G&L Systemhaus, Signapse, Norsk/id3as, and Akamai to support live and on-demand streaming with automatic sign language interpretation, improving accessibility for deaf and hard-of-code viewers.

## International Honour for Excellence

The prestigious **IBC International Honour for Excellence** was presented to **Olympic Broadcasting Services (OBS)**, recognising decades of innovation in live sports production. The award acknowledged advances in UHD, immersive audio, remote production, AI, and cloud technologies that have transformed how the Olympic Games are broadcast to audiences worldwide.

"OBS has consistently pushed the boundaries of what's possible in live production," said the IBC awards committee. "Their work has set the standard for the entire industry."

## Best Technical Paper

The Best Technical Paper award went to **Emma Maier, Jodie Pearton, Anna Stewart, Arina Mnatsakanyan, and Jonathan Freeman** of i2 media research for their work on "reducing interaction barriers for older viewers of internet-delivered TV." The research examined how user interface design and personalisation algorithms can be adapted to make streaming services more accessible to older audiences.

## Accelerator Project of the Year

The ceremony also celebrated the **Accelerator Project of the Year Award** — recognising the collaborative project that demonstrated the most significant impact over the course of the IBC2026 Accelerator programme.

## Award ceremony

Hosted by Sasha Twining, the awards began with a drinks reception at 18:00, with the ceremony starting at 18:30 in Conference Room 1. The evening celebrated the collaborative spirit that defines IBC — bringing together broadcasters, technology vendors, startups, and research institutions to solve real industry challenges.

The ceremony concluded the four-day IBC2026 Innovation Awards programme, which recognised projects that are already deployed and delivering measurable value to the media and entertainment industry.
