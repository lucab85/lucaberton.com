Not every important moment at KubeCon + CloudNativeCon Japan 2026 happened on a keynote slide about AI. Some of the most useful signals came from the **ecosystem announcements** — the partnerships and new projects that change what teams can actually build on top of Kubernetes.

## CNCF + Udemy: training goes mainstream

One announcement I photographed was the **"New Partnership: CNCF + Udemy — Now Available"** slide. It bundles the core Kubernetes certifications — **CKA (Certified Kubernetes Administrator)**, **CKAD (Certified Kubernetes Application Developer)**, and **CKS (Certified Kubernetes Security Specialist)** — with Linux Foundation Education content on Udemy.

Why this matters: the single biggest blocker for AI-on-Kubernetes adoption is *skills*, not GPUs. Making the official cert track available on a mass-market platform lowers the entry barrier for exactly the Japanese enterprises CNCF keeps citing in its adoption studies.

## Akrites: a new supply-chain security project

Another session introduced **Akrites**, a new Linux Foundation open-source project focused on software supply-chain security, announced June 25, 2026. Its founding sponsors are a who's-who of the ecosystem: **AWS, Anthropic, Cisco, Citi, Google, IBM, JPMorgan Chase, Microsoft, GitHub, NVIDIA, OpenAI, Red Hat, and the Rust Foundation**, with the project hosted by the CNCF at akrites.org.

For platform teams, supply-chain security is no longer optional — it is a precondition for running AI workloads in regulated industries. A CNCF-hosted, broadly-backed project is the right home for that work.

## Fujitsu + NVIDIA: the AI platform push

The AI workload shift was quantified in a keynote-level talk, **"A Shift in AI Workloads,"** carrying NVIDIA and Microsoft branding:

- **AI compute by 2026:** inference **85%** vs training **15%**.
- **AI inference power demand projection: 93.3 GW.**

Fujitsu's own session, **"Driving the AI Platform with OSS,"** made the local case: trusted technology through innovation enabled by open-source software. For a Japanese audience, that framing — sovereign, trusted, OSS-built — is the selling point that gets enterprises to commit.

## Takeaway

The talks tell you what is technically possible; the ecosystem moves tell you what will actually be *adoptable*. CNCF + Udemy fixes the skills gap, Akrites hardens the supply chain, and Fujitsu + NVIDIA quantify the AI compute shift. Together they are the connective tissue of the conference.
