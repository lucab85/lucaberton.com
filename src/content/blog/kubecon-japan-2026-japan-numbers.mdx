One of the most grounding sessions at KubeCon + CloudNativeCon Japan 2026 was simply titled **"Japan's Cloud Native Footprint, By the Numbers."** It pulled hard data from the CNCF *State of Cloud Native Development in Japan* report, and the figures reframe how we should think about the region.

## The headline numbers

- **950,000** cloud-native developers in Japan as of Q1 2026.
- **41%** of Japanese developers are cloud-native — above the **39% global average**.
- That share grew from **24%** in Q1 2024 to **41%** in Q1 2026.
- Roughly **100,000** of Japan's cloud-native developers work on AI.

The trajectory is the story: in two years, Japan went from below-global to above-global cloud-native penetration. That is the backdrop for every AI-on-Kubernetes talk at the conference.

## Production stories: Cilium at Cybozu

The "Already in Production in Japan" session made the numbers concrete. **Cybozu** — a major Japanese enterprise SaaS company — migrated **1,000+ bare-metal servers** from VMs to Kubernetes using **Cilium**, and has run Cilium in production for **4+ years**. For teams still debating whether cloud-native networking is "ready," that is a four-year, thousand-server answer.

## Identity at scale: Keycloak for an e-pharmacy

A second case study covered an **Infosys** client — a Japanese pharmaceutical company — that used **Keycloak** to unify authentication for **2M+ users** across an e-pharmacy platform serving **750+ pharmacies** and **200,000+ patients**, while meeting strict Japanese health-data security regulations.

It is a reminder that "cloud native in Japan" is not only hyperscalers and GPUs. It is regulated, document-heavy industries (pharma, finance) moving real workloads onto open-source identity and networking primitives.

## Why this matters for platform teams

The takeaway for anyone building platforms: Japan's adoption curve means the talent pool and the production precedent already exist. The bottlenecks are now *infrastructure skills and security confidence* — which is exactly why the CNCF + Udemy partnership and the Akrites supply-chain project (covered elsewhere in this series) showed up on the same stage.
