The opening keynote at **KubeCon + CloudNativeCon Japan 2026** was titled **"Keep Cloud Native Moving: Building Japan's Platform for Open Innovation."** Chris Aniszczyk (CTO, Cloud & Infrastructure, Linux Foundation) and Jonathan Bryce (Executive Director, Cloud & Infrastructure, Linux Foundation) took the main stage at PACIFICO Yokohama, and the framing was deliberately about momentum rather than a single product reveal.

![Opening keynote title slide Keep Cloud Native Moving: Building Japan's Platform for Open Innovation with Chris Aniszczyk and Jonathan Bryce](/blog/events/kubecon-japan-2026-opening-keynote/keep-cloud-native-moving-title.jpg)

*The opening keynote title slide on the main stage.*

## Why "open innovation" matters here

Japan's open-source story has real data behind it. In the Linux Foundation's *State of Open Source Japan 2025* study, **69% of Japanese firms** reported gaining more business value through open source. In CNCF's announcement for the event, Bryce tied that directly to the AI moment: inference is becoming "the largest compute use case in human history," and **66% of organizations already use Kubernetes as the operating system for AI.**

That is the throughline of the whole event — not "should we adopt cloud native?" but "how do we standardize the stack so AI is reliable and scalable?"

## The sponsors behind the stage

The keynote also surfaced the **Platinum Sponsors** on the main screen: **Canonical**, **Fujitsu**, and **Hitachi**. For a Japanese edition, that local enterprise backing is the signal that matters — these are the companies actually running cloud-native infrastructure at national scale, and their presence on the keynote stage reflects where the adoption is happening.

## The recurring message

A line repeated across the morning's slides: **"Let's keep cloud native moving."** It showed up on the welcome screen, the community session, and the closing calls to action.

![Keynote slide reading Let's keep cloud native moving with both speakers on stage](/blog/events/kubecon-japan-2026-opening-keynote/lets-keep-cloud-native-moving.jpg)

*"Let's keep cloud native moving."*

In practice, "keeping it moving" meant three things the rest of the program then expanded on:

1. **Standardize the AI stack** so inference is portable and conformant across environments.
2. **Invest in the community** — code *and* non-code contributions, local chapters, and advocacy.
3. **Build the platform for open innovation** — Japan-specific, but built on the same CNCF projects everyone else uses.

![Keynote slide Extending the CARE Program: achieving your CKS extends your CKA](/blog/events/kubecon-japan-2026-opening-keynote/care-program-cks-cka.jpg)

*A keynote slide on certification: "Extending the CARE Program", where achieving the CKS extends the CKA.*

## Takeaway

The keynote did not try to be flashy. It set a direction: Kubernetes is the substrate for AI, open source is the accelerant, and Japan's enterprises are all-in. The sessions that followed spent the rest of the day proving it.
