The opening keynote at **KubeCon + CloudNativeCon Japan 2026** was titled **"Keep Cloud Native Moving: Building Japan's Platform for Open Innovation."** Chris Aniszczyk (CTO, CNCF) and Jonathan Bryce (Executive Director, Cloud & Infrastructure, Linux Foundation) took the main stage at PACIFICO Yokohama, and the framing was deliberately about momentum rather than a single product reveal.

## Why "open innovation" matters here

Japan's open-source story has real data behind it. In the Linux Foundation's *State of Open Source Japan 2025* study, **69% of Japanese firms** reported gaining more business value through open source. Bryce's keynote tied that directly to the AI moment: inference is becoming "the largest compute use case in human history," and **66% of organizations already use Kubernetes as the operating system for AI.**

That is the throughline of the whole event — not "should we adopt cloud native?" but "how do we standardize the stack so AI is reliable and scalable?"

## The sponsors behind the stage

The keynote also surfaced the **Platinum Sponsors** on the main screen: **Canonical**, **Fujitsu**, and **Hitachi**. For a Japanese edition, that local enterprise backing is the signal that matters — these are the companies actually running cloud-native infrastructure at national scale, and their presence on the keynote stage reflects where the adoption is happening.

## The recurring message

A line repeated across the morning's slides: **"Let's keep cloud native moving."** It showed up on the welcome screen, the community session, and the closing calls to action.

In practice, "keeping it moving" meant three things the rest of the program then expanded on:

1. **Standardize the AI stack** so inference is portable and conformant across environments.
2. **Invest in the community** — code *and* non-code contributions, local chapters, and advocacy.
3. **Build the platform for open innovation** — Japan-specific, but built on the same CNCF projects everyone else uses.

## Takeaway

The keynote did not try to be flashy. It set a direction: Kubernetes is the substrate for AI, open source is the accelerant, and Japan's enterprises are all-in. The sessions that followed spent the rest of the day proving it.
