Around midday at [Red Hat Summit: Connect 2026 Utrecht](/blog/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/), I sat down with **Marcel Timmer**, Country Managing Director of Red Hat in the Netherlands. A few hours earlier he had opened the keynote with the number that one in four Dutch organizations face an exposure window of more than 24 hours for critical vulnerabilities. We talked for eight minutes. Below is what he said, in his words where my recording is clear and in summary where it is not.

## A year that went fast

We started with how fast the year went. Even the tools people used a year ago are not keeping pace, and Red Hat had just released OpenShift 5. His question: "can we absorb all those changes fast enough? That's a big question."

The biggest organizations in the country were in the building. "We had 1,200 people registering," he said, "but I'm not sure how many people actually showed up."

## The questions he hears now

The first kind of question, he said, comes from Mythos and AI-driven vulnerability discovery: people want to know how to find out what problems they have. Big customers don't always have access to Mythos or that type of scan, so they look for alternatives, and they already ask Red Hat to help with the vulnerabilities that turn up. "We didn't have that conversation a year ago at all."

The second is about AI deployments: can Red Hat help make sure they are safe? There is a lot of concern about intellectual property leaking to the big models.

![Marcel Timmer answering a question, his hands open, Luca Berton listening](/blog/events/marcel-timmer-interview-red-hat-summit-connect-2026/timmer-interview-timmer-answers.jpg)

I asked whether that is more about guardrails or about sovereignty. "Sovereignty and guardrail are a bit of the same, right?" For Red Hat, he said, sovereignty is embedded in what the company is and does. What matters is "the question behind the question": what customers want to do, and how they manage their risk. AI is now a boardroom topic, and the board wants to know three things: "Is it safe? Is it auditable? Am I okay if I do this?"

## Agents, and shadow AI

Are many organizations running AI agents in production? A lot of customers are still in the experimentation phase, Marcel said, and are now moving to production; that is where the questions come from. Then there is shadow AI: people installing their own tools on their desktops, maybe with a policy, maybe without.

## Virtualization, still

![Luca Berton talking, Marcel Timmer listening, during the part of the conversation about OpenShift Virtualization](/blog/events/marcel-timmer-interview-red-hat-summit-connect-2026/timmer-interview-virtualization.jpg)

Virtualization keeps coming back, two years in a row now. Organizations want a good alternative: one that is cheaper, more future-proof, or simply gives them a choice, because they are locked in today and that is not easy to undo.

I told him what surprised me at a workshop: OpenShift Virtualization now does live migration, the way we are used to with other vendors. He went further: compared with four years ago, there has been so much innovation that people who think "it's still the same" are wrong. The path he sees is that organizations adopt OpenShift for virtualization, then add workloads as containers, and then start modernizing the virtual estate. The keynote had a slide full of OpenShift Virtualization customers to make the same point.

## OpenShift 5 and predictability

With OpenShift 5, Red Hat wants to make it easier to deploy and to upgrade, with in-place upgrades. What caught my eye in OpenShift 5 is the predictable maintenance window, and a longer one than in OpenShift 4. Marcel said that came from customer feedback: customers want predictability, and many software vendors are unpredictable in how they treat them. "When we release it, we already show you, this is how it's going to look like in the coming years."

He also brought up skills. With this much change, he said, everyone has to keep learning: trainings, books (he kindly mentioned mine), and the community and partners leveling up together.

## Delivering AI safely

![Marcel Timmer explaining how to deliver AI safely in an enterprise, Luca Berton listening](/blog/events/marcel-timmer-interview-red-hat-summit-connect-2026/timmer-interview-ai-safely.jpg)

My last question: how do I deliver AI in an enterprise environment safely? Half joking, he said he would normally hand out my card. Then his answer started with people, not products: come and talk to Red Hat, its partners and the ecosystem. The tooling exists, he said, whether you use your own models or small language models, but it goes hand in hand with implementation, change management and the people who do the work. Installing OpenShift AI is not what delivers the value on its own: managing it, taking it further and making it a way of working is. And his closing line: "You should not underestimate the human in the loop that needs to work with this as well."

Thanks to Marcel for the time, on a busy day.
