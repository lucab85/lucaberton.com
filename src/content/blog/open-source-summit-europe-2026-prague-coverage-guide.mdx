**Open Source Summit Europe 2026** took place on 7–9 October at the Prague Congress Centre, together with the Embedded Linux Conference and next to the Linux Plumbers Conference. I attended as a media partner for my ten-minute interview show [Udienza](https://www.udienza.com/), with Marcin Czupryniak behind the camera.

![Luca Berton and Marcin Czupryniak of Udienza on the first morning in Prague](/blog/events/open-source-summit-2026/oss-eu-2026-udienza-crew-morning.jpg)

One week produced more material than one recap can hold, so I split it by theme. This page is the map. Start with the [event recap](/blog/open-source-summit-europe-2026-prague-recap/) for the overview, then pick the theme you care about.

## Europe, sovereignty and the state of open source

The opening keynotes set the tone: Europe writes a large share of the world's open source but has a much smaller share of the governance seats and funding.

- [State of Open Source in Europe 2026: Numbers from Prague](/blog/oss-europe-2026-state-of-open-source-in-europe-report/). The Linux Foundation Research report as presented on stage: AI coding, the 59% vs 37% code-versus-governance gap, and Europe's share of Linux, Kubernetes and Zephyr.
- [94% of European Organisations Use or Pilot AI Coding Tools](/blog/open-source-summit-prague-2026-ai-coding-94-percent/). What the headline AI adoption number means for engineering teams and maintainers.
- [Digital Sovereignty Is an Open Source Governance Problem](/blog/open-source-summit-prague-2026-digital-sovereignty-governance/). Why sovereignty depends on governance, upstream contribution and fewer private forks.
- [Open Source at a Crossroads: Sovereignty in Prague](/blog/oss-europe-2026-open-source-crossroads-sovereignty/). Nithya Ruff and Johan Linåker on geopolitics and public stewardship, plus the "Who Owns the Stack?" press panel.
- [OpenGrid: Open Source for the Power Grid Bottleneck](/blog/oss-europe-2026-opengrid-open-source-grid-planning/). The Linux Foundation's new project for grid planning, launched in Prague.
- [Sovereign AI Models: Teuken, Apertus and OpenEuroLLM](/blog/sovereign-ai-models-teuken-apertus-openeurollm/). The national and regional open models named in the sovereignty keynote, and how to choose.

## Security in the age of AI-speed exploits

- [AI-Speed Exploits: Open Secure AI Alliance and OSERA](/blog/oss-europe-2026-ai-speed-exploits-open-secure-ai-alliance-osera/). A 42-advisory spike in OpenStack, the SAFE incident exchange, and six banks mutualising patches.
- [FINOS OSERA at Prague 2026: Patch Once, Verify Everywhere](/blog/open-source-summit-prague-2026-finos-osera-remediation-standards/). A closer look at OSERA's open Remediation Standards: provenance, VEX and compatibility.

## AI agents, trust and isolation

- [Trusted AI Agents in Prague: Cedar, Dogwood and Strands](/blog/oss-europe-2026-trusted-agentic-workloads-cedar-dogwood/). Laura Tacho's AWS keynote: the trust gap, identity, policy, limits and audit, and history-aware policy with Dogwood.
- [Isolating AI Agents: Edera, Coder and OpenBao](/blog/oss-europe-2026-isolating-ai-agents-edera-coder-openbao/). Hypervisor isolation, cloud workspaces for coding agents, and per-tenant secrets.
- [Docsy Moves to the Linux Foundation: Docs for AI Agents](/blog/oss-europe-2026-docsy-linux-foundation-docs-for-ai-agents/). Erin McKean's keynote, `llms.txt`, Markdown next to HTML, and measuring agent-friendly docs.

## Linux, operating systems and embedded

- [Kernel Maintainers in Prague: Greg KH and Arnaldo Melo](/blog/oss-europe-2026-kernel-maintainers-greg-kh-arnaldo-melo/). Kernel sustainability, 20 years of pahole and BTF, and b4.
- [The OS Layer in the AI Era: openKylin, openEuler, Flatcar](/blog/oss-europe-2026-operating-systems-openkylin-openeuler-flatcar/). KV-cache tiering in the OS, AI-native desktops, and immutable Kubernetes hosts.
- [Embedded Linux in Prague: TI, Igalia WPE and RISC-V](/blog/oss-europe-2026-embedded-linux-ti-igalia-risc-v/). FPGA test fabrics, the new WPEPlatform API, RISC-V and chip data for AI.

## Five takeaways from the week

1. **Participation beats consumption.** The report's own conclusion: participation in openly governed open source is what creates sovereignty and return on investment.
2. **The security bottleneck has moved.** AI finds vulnerabilities at machine speed. Absorbing the fixes, with evidence, is now the hard part.
3. **Agents need standard controls.** Identity, policy, limits and audit should be shared building blocks, not reinvented in every framework.
4. **The OS is back in the conversation.** From KV-cache tiering to immutable nodes, the operating system decides cost and security for AI workloads.
5. **Linux at 35 is a people story.** The kernel, the foundations and the hallway track all run on maintainers' time. Protect it.
