The **Open Source Summit Europe 2026** took place October 7-9 in Prague, Czechia — three days of keynotes, technical sessions, and networking across the Cloud & Orchestration, Open AI + Data, Digital Trust, Embedded Linux, and Safety-Critical Software tracks. Co-located with the Linux Plumbers Conference and Embedded Linux Conference, the event brought together maintainers, developers, and community leaders from across the open source ecosystem.

The central theme was clear: **open source is the foundation for European digital sovereignty**, particularly in the age of AI. From cloud sovereignty keynotes to agentic workload standards, the conversations I captured on the floor reflected a community actively shaping the future of decentralized technology.

## Arriving in Prague

After landing in Prague, I took the tram to the venue — the Congress Hall in the Arkády Pankrác complex. The city's red-tiled roofs and the Vltava river provide a fitting backdrop for a summit focused on European technological independence.

The event opened with a Code of Conduct briefing from The Linux Foundation, setting the tone for three days of collaboration. The Congress Hall filled quickly as attendees picked up badges on Floor 0 and headed upstairs to Floor 2 for the keynotes.

## Keynote: Sovereignty Requires Real Choice

Tarek Becker, CTO at SAP, opened the Thursday keynote block with **"Sovereignty Requires Real Choice"** — a direct challenge to the assumption that cloud provider lock-in is inevitable. His argument: open source doesn't just *enable* choice, it is choice. Without it, digital sovereignty is a mirage.

This set the stage for what became the summit's unifying thread.

## Agentic AI and Open Standards

Laura Tacho, Senior Principal Technologist at AWS, presented on **"Open Standards for Trusted Agentic Workloads"** — a framework for defining interoperability standards as autonomous AI agents move from experimental to production. The talk addressed how enterprises can deploy agentic systems without sacrificing security or vendor neutrality.

I caught the intro on camera as she was introduced to the Congress Hall audience.

![Slide: "Open Standards for Trusted Agentic Workloads" by Laura Tacho, AWS](/blog/events/open-source-summit-2026/oss-eu-2026-laura-tacho-slide.jpg)
*Laura Tacho (AWS) introducing "Open Standards for Trusted Agentic Workloads" — a critical conversation as enterprises begin deploying autonomous AI agents.*

## The Future of Open Source Security

Jamie Thomas, IBM's Chief Client Innovation Officer for Enterprise Security, delivered the **Thursday keynote on open source security in the age of AI**. Her message: AI is both a weapon and a shield in the security landscape. Attackers use AI to find vulnerabilities faster, but defenders can use the same technology to automate patch management, threat hunting, and supply chain verification.

A recurring theme in the hallways was the emergence of the **Open Secure AI Alliance** — an initiative focused on "developing a shared, open defensive infrastructure for the AI era." The group is pooling resources to deal with incoming security reports and building collaborative defense mechanisms around AI systems.

![Slide: "Organizing the defense" — Open Secure AI Alliance](/blog/events/open-source-summit-2026/oss-eu-2026-open-secure-ai.jpg)
*The Open Secure AI Alliance presentation on collaborative defense for the AI era.*

## Cloud Sovereignty: A Panel Discussion

One of the most attended sessions was the **Cloud Sovereignty** panel, which unpacked the eight SOV (Sovereignty) criteria that European organizations are using to evaluate cloud providers. The discussion covered everything from data residency requirements to open source licensing as a sovereignty lever.

The panel featured six experts from across the European tech ecosystem — a reflection of how sovereignty has become a cross-cutting concern, not a niche topic.

![Cloud Sovereignty panel at Open Source Summit Europe 2026](/blog/events/open-source-summit-2026/oss-eu-2026-cloud-sovereignty-panel.jpg)

## The Solutions Showcase Floor

The Solutions Showcase on Floors 2 and 3 featured booths from dozens of sponsors — from Platinum partners like Red Hat, OpenSearch, and OpenEuler to Silver sponsors like the CNCF, OpenSSF, and Zephyr Project.

I caught up with **Arnaldo Melo** from Red Hat at the Linux Plumbers Conference area, which is co-located with Open Source Summit. The Plumbers Conference focuses on the low-level "plumbing" of the Linux kernel — the infrastructure that keeps everything running.

![Luca Berton with Arnaldo Melo at the Linux Plumbers Conference booth](/blog/events/open-source-summit-2026/oss-eu-2026-arnaldo-melo-selfie.jpg)

Later in the afternoon, I ran into **Marcin Czupryniak** (udienza) and colleagues at the GUAC booth. GUAC — the Graph for Understanding Artifact Composition — maps relationships between software components, SBOMs, and security metadata. Their presentation highlighted how supply chain security is becoming a first-class concern for open source projects.

The **OpenSSF** duck logo was everywhere on the floor, and the **Framework** booth's "Fix consumer electronics" message drew crowds who are interested in repairable, modular hardware as part of the broader open source movement.

## Technical Sessions: Linux, Zephyr, and Beyond

The schedule showed 78 Embedded Linux Conference sessions, 46 Zephyr Developer Summit talks, and 26 PX4 Dev Summit presentations — alongside 24 Cloud & Orchestration and 23 Open AI & Data sessions. Key themes included:

- **Runtime power management for IOMMUs** (Google)
- **Zephyr in automotive mixed-OS systems** (Renesas, NXP)
- **Over-the-air updates for immutable filesystems** (Northern.tech)
- **Multi-stream camera sensors in Linux** (Texas Instruments)
- **Benchmarking OSS agent memory on Kubernetes** (Dynatrace)

The breadth of technical depth — from kernel internals to agentic AI infrastructure — reinforced the sense that this wasn't just a "talk about AI" conference. It was about building the systems that will run the next decade of open source software.

## Sponsors and Ecosystem

Diamond sponsors included AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, openKylin, and Valkey. Platinum-level support came from OpenEuler, OpenSearch, and Red Hat. The full sponsor list — from CIP and CNCF to Zephyr Project — reflected the breadth of the open source ecosystem gathering in Prague.

## What This Means for Open Source

The dominant narrative in Prague was **digital sovereignty through open source**. Unlike past conferences where "sovereignty" was primarily a policy concern, here it was framed as an engineering imperative. Every keynote, panel, and hallway conversation circled back to the same question: how do we build distributed, trustworthy, AI-native systems without creating new forms of vendor lock-in?

The answer, the community seems to agree, lies in open standards, shared infrastructure, and collaborative defense — not in any single company's product.
