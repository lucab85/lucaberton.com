The first keynote block of **Open Source Summit Europe 2026** opened with data, not slogans. After the welcome from Thierry Carrez, General Manager of Linux Foundation Europe, the Congress Hall got a tour of the new **2026 State of Open Source in Europe** report from Linux Foundation Research. The subtitle is *Navigating AI and Digital Sovereignty Through Open Governance*.

I was in the hall as a media partner for my interview show [Udienza](https://www.udienza.com/), and I photographed most of the slides. The numbers are worth more than a summary, so here they are, with my reading of what they mean for engineering teams.

![The 2026 State of Open Source in Europe report cover on the Congress Hall screen](/blog/events/open-source-summit-2026/oss-eu-2026-state-of-open-source-europe-report.jpg)
*The report is authored by Dr. Mirko Boehm and Paula Grzegorzewska (Linux Foundation Europe) and Marco Gerosa (Northern Arizona University), with a foreword by Greg Kroah-Hartman. It is sponsored by the NeoNephos Foundation.*

![Luca Berton and Marcin Czupryniak of Udienza in the Congress Hall during the opening keynotes](/blog/events/open-source-summit-2026/oss-eu-2026-udienza-crew-keynote-seats.jpg)
*With Marcin Czupryniak, who filmed the Udienza interviews, in the Congress Hall for the opening keynotes.*

## AI coding tools are increasing open source use, not replacing it

The first slide went straight at the question everybody in the room had: do AI coding assistants make open source less necessary? According to the survey, the opposite is happening.

![Slide: AI coding tools and open source use in Europe](/blog/events/open-source-summit-2026/oss-eu-2026-europe-ai-coding-open-source.jpg)

- **94%** of European organisations are already using or piloting generative AI coding tools.
- **48%** report *more* open source use because of AI coding assistants, against just **5%** reporting less.
- **76%** say AI tools help their team get more value from the open source they already run.
- Only **28%** expect AI coding tools to reduce their reliance on open source in future.

This matches what I see with clients. An assistant writes glue code. It does not write a database, a kernel, or a container runtime. The more code an assistant produces, the more dependencies get pulled in, and the more your supply chain matters. I covered the production side of that in [AI agent guardrails for production](/blog/ai-agent-guardrails-production/). For what the 94% adoption figure means for engineering teams and maintainers, see [94% of European organisations use or pilot AI coding tools](/blog/open-source-summit-prague-2026-ai-coding-94-percent/).

## Europe contributes code, but not governance

The most important slide of the morning, in my opinion, was the one about the gap between writing code and having a say.

![Slide: European code contribution versus governance participation](/blog/events/open-source-summit-2026/oss-eu-2026-europe-contribution-governance.jpg)

- **59%** of European organisations contribute code upstream, but only **37%** participate in open source project governance.
- Open source returns **4.1x** benefit-to-cost on average, rising from **3.6x** with no formal governance to **4.6x** at the most mature governance level.
- **83%** of contributors get advance warning before a breaking change lands, one of the concrete paybacks of participation beyond code.
- Foundation-hosted, neutrally governed projects are Europe's preferred model at **52%**, eight percentage points ahead of the rest of the world.

The keynote then put the gap on one slide:

![Slide: closing the code and governance gap, 59% vs 37% and 38% vs 15%](/blog/events/open-source-summit-2026/oss-eu-2026-code-governance-gap.jpg)

European contributions account for **38% of code activity** in the projects studied, but only **15% of funding**. Europe does the work and leaves the steering wheel to someone else. I unpack why that makes sovereignty a governance problem in [Digital Sovereignty Is an Open Source Governance Problem](/blog/open-source-summit-prague-2026-digital-sovereignty-governance/).

## How much of the stack Europe actually writes

The report also measured Europe's share of code contribution in some of the projects everyone runs:

![Slide: Europe's code contribution by project](/blog/events/open-source-summit-2026/oss-eu-2026-europe-code-contribution.jpg)

| Project | What it is | European share |
|---|---|---|
| Zephyr | Real-time OS for embedded devices | 50.5% |
| Linux kernel | The kernel itself | 43.9% |
| OpenStack | Open cloud infrastructure | 41.5% |
| OpenTelemetry | Observability standard | 35.5% |
| Kubernetes | Container orchestration | 34.9% |

If you run Kubernetes on Linux with OpenTelemetry, a third or more of that code was written in Europe. That is a strong position to negotiate from, and most organisations do not use it.

## Sovereignty is a board-level topic now

![Slide: digital sovereignty statistics for European organisations](/blog/events/open-source-summit-2026/oss-eu-2026-europe-sovereignty-stats.jpg)

- **94%** of European organisations think digital sovereignty is strategically important, with **61%** calling it very important.
- Security and privacy lead as the highest driver of sovereignty, at **71%**.
- Advancing national and regional interests through sovereignty is felt most acutely in Europe, **12 percentage points** higher than the rest of the world.
- The EU **Tech Sovereignty Package** is described as the first major global legislative bet on open source as a pillar of strategic autonomy, backed by a planned **€2bn** over seven years.

## "Participation in openly-governed open source is the solution"

My favourite slide of the morning was a piece of crossed-out text. It is the most honest summary of the sovereignty debate I have seen.

![Slide: "Participation in openly-governed open source is the solution"](/blog/events/open-source-summit-2026/oss-eu-2026-participation-is-the-solution.jpg)

1. ~~Open source is the solution.~~ It gives availability, transparency and a forking safety valve. But single-vendor open source is just trading one dependency for another.
2. ~~Openly-governed open source is the solution.~~ No single organisation controls the software, and everyone can join. But can you have effective control without project expertise?
3. **Participation in openly-governed open source is the solution.**

The report's conclusion slide called it "deepen participation in the global commons": engagement, contribution (support upstream maintainers as they adapt to the changes AI brings), and policy that treats regulation as an opportunity.

## What I would do with this as an engineering leader

1. **Count your governance seats, not just your commits.** If your team contributes to a project you depend on, find out whether anyone attends its steering, TSC or SIG meetings. The data says the return goes up with governance maturity.
2. **Prefer foundation-hosted projects for critical dependencies.** Single-vendor open source can be relicensed. The forks of the last few years, from OpenTofu to OpenBao to Valkey, all came from that.
3. **Fund the maintainers you rely on.** 38% of the code against 15% of the funding is not sustainable.
4. **Treat AI coding assistants as a supply chain multiplier.** More generated code means more dependencies to track. Your SBOM and patch process matter more in 2026, not less.
