On Wednesday I went to NBC Nieuwegein, next to Utrecht, for **Red Hat Summit: Connect 2026 Utrecht**, Red Hat's one-day event for the Netherlands. It ran from 08:00 to 18:00: an opening keynote at 09:30, four rounds of breakout sessions, demos in "silent theaters", and a closing reception in the expo hall. I took my photos during the first part of the keynote, from 09:20 to 10:05, so these notes follow its slides.

![Luca Berton pointing at the Dell Technologies and ITQ logos on the sponsors wall, with Intel, Kangaroot and HCS Company below](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/rhsc-nl-2026-sponsors.jpg)

The sponsors wall explains part of what came later. Intel was the lead sponsor, Dell and ITQ the diamond ones, with Conclusion, HCS and Kangaroot at gold level. ITQ ended up on the main stage.

![Luca Berton in the keynote hall before the first speaker, the stage lit in red behind him](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/rhsc-nl-2026-hall.jpg)

## Marcel Timmer: one in four

![Marcel Timmer, Country Managing Director at Red Hat NL, on the keynote stage, his name on the screen behind him](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/rhsc-nl-2026-timmer.jpg)

Marcel Timmer, Country Managing Director at Red Hat NL, opened the day, thanked the sponsors, and then put up a slide called "Navigating the shift in our market". It had three boxes, and I could read two of them: "Accelerating AI adoption" and "Evolving risk management".

The next slide carried the number I took home from his part:

![Marcel Timmer next to the Exposure window slide: 1 in 4 organizations in the Netherlands face a more than 24 hour exposure window for critical vulnerabilities](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/rhsc-nl-2026-exposure.jpg)

**1 in 4 organizations in the Netherlands face an exposure window of more than 24 hours for critical vulnerabilities.** The footnote defined it: the mean time to deploy a critical software fix across affected production systems is 24.21 hours. Keep that figure in mind for the end of this post.

## Andrew Brown: what do I build on?

![Andrew Brown, Senior Vice President and Chief Revenue Officer at Red Hat, with his name on the screen](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/rhsc-nl-2026-brown.jpg)

Andrew Brown, Senior Vice President and Chief Revenue Officer at Red Hat, spoke for most of the part I photographed. His first slide asked "What do I build on?" and answered in three rows: proprietary systems gave way to Linux and Red Hat Enterprise Linux, containers to Red Hat OpenShift, and AI agents to Red Hat AI.

![Andrew Brown beside the What do I build on? slide: Linux leads to Red Hat Enterprise Linux, containers to OpenShift, AI agents to Red Hat AI](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/rhsc-nl-2026-build-on.jpg)

Then came a slide on **AI tokenomics in the public cloud**, with three curves: AI consumption and AI total costs going up, AI token costs going down. The price of a token falls, and the bill still grows, because consumption grows faster.

![Andrew Brown on stage, the AI tokenomics in the public cloud slide on both screens: AI consumption and total costs rising, AI token costs falling](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/rhsc-nl-2026-tokenomics.jpg)

## Private AI, with three cases from ITQ

![Jeffrey Kusters walking on stage to shake hands with Andrew Brown, his name on the screen](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/rhsc-nl-2026-kusters.jpg)

Andrew Brown handed over to Jeffrey Kusters, CTO at ITQ, for "Private AI: From Concept to Production", subtitled "Bringing enterprise AI to sovereign data with Red Hat OpenShift AI". Three cards, three kinds of customer:

- **Telecom and sovereignty: SETAR (Aruba).** An island-native telecom platform built on OpenShift AI, with "in-region governance and token cost control".
- **Healthcare research: Catharina Hospital (Eindhoven).** More than 125 clinicians and researchers on on-premise OpenShift AI: "sovereign patient data and local GPU scale".
- **Real-time edge AI: MP Motorsport.** A Virtual Race Engineer analyzing multi-lap telemetry, "compact AI coaching without a data center truck".

![Andrew Brown and Jeffrey Kusters of ITQ on stage in front of the Private AI: From Concept to Production slide, with the SETAR, Catharina Hospital and MP Motorsport cards](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/rhsc-nl-2026-private-ai.jpg)

The slide ended with a "dive deeper" strip pointing to the rest of the day: a breakout on the Catharina Hospital case, a race simulator demo at the ITQ and Dell stand, and a workshop called "Prompt to Production". In the agenda, the hospital session is at 14:00 and in Dutch, with two people from the hospital and ITQ's Johan van Amersfoort.

## Red Hat AI, layer by layer

Andrew Brown then walked up a five-layer slide for Red Hat AI, highlighting one layer at a time:

- **Any accelerator:** NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure.
- **AI infrastructure:** Red Hat Enterprise Linux as an "integrated agentic OS", the "foundation of AI system", with "Day 0 hardware support".

![Andrew Brown with the Red Hat AI stack slide, the AI infrastructure layer highlighted: Red Hat Enterprise Linux as an integrated agentic OS](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/rhsc-nl-2026-ai-infrastructure.jpg)

- **Model services:** Model-as-a-Service and an AI gateway, because "enterprises need many models", with "Red Hat validated models on Day 0".
- **Agent services:** bring your own agents, AgentOps, agent identity, agent lifecycle, MCP services and tools, and agent observability.

![Andrew Brown with the Red Hat AI stack slide, agent services highlighted: bring your own agents, AgentOps, agent identity, lifecycle, MCP services and observability](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/rhsc-nl-2026-agent-services.jpg)

The fifth layer, inference services, sits between the model and the infrastructure layers; I did not catch its highlight.

## Automation Orchestrator for Ansible

The product news came with a slide titled "Introducing the Automation Orchestrator for Ansible: the trusted execution layer for AI-driven IT operations", and a workflow it called "one governed workflow, end-to-end":

![Andrew Brown in front of the Automation Orchestrator for Ansible slide: a workflow from three vulnerability alerts, through human review, to a remediation job](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/rhsc-nl-2026-orchestrator.jpg)

Three vulnerability alerts (from IBM Instana, ServiceNow and Splunk, each an Event-Driven Ansible trigger) open a ServiceNow ticket through an Ansible Automation Platform job. An analysis step follows, then a human review with an approve or reject gate and a timeout of 3600 seconds, and only after approval an "Execute Remediation" job with the "Patch and Restart" template. The slide summed it up under two ticks: **humans approve, automation executes.**

Red Hat's own agenda has a session on the same idea at 11:30, "Before the exploit strikes", by Jürgen Buddy Hoffmann, Ansible Platform Leader: "AI agent swarms can weaponize vulnerabilities in 30 minutes", and Ansible Automation Platform compresses remediation "from weeks to hours with human-in-the-loop control".

## The ground has shifted

The last slides I photographed changed topic to engineering and security. I could not read a name on screen for this part. The keynote's speaker list includes Vincent Danen, Red Hat's Vice President of Product Security, and the subject is his area, but I leave the attribution to the agenda.

![The ground has shifted slide: 90% of developers use AI, 98% more pull requests merged, 75% of engineers by 2028, 40% of enterprise apps with agents by the end of 2026](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/rhsc-nl-2026-ground-shifted.jpg)

"The ground has shifted" said that agentic development is no longer on the horizon: AI is already doing the engineering work, at scale. Four cards, each with its source on the slide:

- **90%** of developers now use AI in their work, up 14 points in a single year (Google DORA, 2025).
- **98%** more pull requests merged by AI-assisted developers than by their peers (Faros AI telemetry, 2025).
- **75%** of engineers will use AI coding assistants by 2028, from under 10% in 2023 (Gartner forecast).
- **40%** of enterprise apps will embed AI agents by the end of 2026, from under 5% in 2024 (Gartner forecast).

Next, "The Mythos Moment: when discovery outpaces defense":

![The Mythos Moment slide: four cards on vulnerability discovery at machine speed, remediation capacity, the response bottleneck and exposure management](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/rhsc-nl-2026-mythos.jpg)

- **AI discovers vulnerabilities at machine speed.** Frontier models find critical zero-day vulnerabilities autonomously, including bugs hidden for 27 years, at under $50 each.
- **Discovery exceeds capacity.** "Discovery is exponential. Remediation is linear." Less than 1% of AI-discovered vulnerabilities have been patched.
- **Discovery is no longer the bottleneck; response is.** 4.8 million unfilled cyber roles, growing 19% year over year, and the National Vulnerability Database shifting to selective enrichment.
- **Shift to exposure management.** Published CVEs are up more than 520% since 2016; the cost of attacks in 2025 was about $60 billion, projected to reach $138 billion by 2031.

These are the slide's numbers, not mine, and I have not checked them against their sources.

The last one I captured was a chart, "From Vulnerability to Exploitation", under the title "AI is accelerating the security vulnerability landscape":

![Chart of the mean time to exploit from CVE disclosure to confirmed exploitation, falling from 2.3 years in 2018 to 18 hours in 2026](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-netherlands-2026/rhsc-nl-2026-time-to-exploit.jpg)

It plots the mean time to exploit, the gap between a CVE's disclosure and confirmed exploitation, from **2.3 years in 2018**, through 10.8 months in 2021, to 56 days in 2024 and **18 hours in 2026**, with the zones labelled months, days and hours. The slide says it is based on 3,532 CVE-exploit pairs from CISA KEV and VulnCheck KEV and XDB, and cites zerodayclock.com and a report titled "The AI Vulnerability Storm: Building a Mythos-ready Security Program".

Put that next to Marcel Timmer's first number: an exposure window of 24 hours at the average Dutch organization, and an average time to exploit that this chart puts at 18 hours. That was the thread of the keynote for me: faster discovery, and a response that still needs people, which is why the orchestrator slide made a point of showing where the humans approve.

## What else was on

My photos stop at 10:05, so I leave the breakouts to the [official agenda](https://www.redhat.com/en/summit/connect/emea/utrecht-2026#agenda). A few that caught my eye, in the order of the day:

- 11:30, Red Hat: "Before the exploit strikes", with Ansible Automation Platform and human-in-the-loop remediation.
- 11:30 to 14:00, ITQ and Dell: a hackathon, "From Prompt to Production: build an app with AI, deploy it on OpenShift".
- 14:00, Red Hat: "From CVE to Production Fix: Upgrade-Free Remediation with Red Hat Lightwell Network".
- 14:00, ITQ: "Why Catharina Hospital Chose Open Source for Healthcare AI", in Dutch.
- 15:15, Vincent Danen, Red Hat: "The sky is not falling, but you still need an umbrella: moving from hype to execution", on AI-driven vulnerability discovery and why mass patching is not the answer.
- 15:15, Red Hat: "What's new in OpenShift 5?" and "Who's using your AI? Usage, costs, and control on Red Hat OpenShift AI".

Thanks to the Red Hat Netherlands team and to the sponsors for the day.
