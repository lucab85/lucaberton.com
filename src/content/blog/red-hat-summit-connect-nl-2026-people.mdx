Every good conference has two things happening at once: the sessions on the stage, and the *community* in the hall. Here are the human details of Summit: Connect 2026 Utrecht.

## The Tech Tribes booth

One of the friendliest corners of the venue was the **HCS company — Tech Tribes** networking area. Tech Tribes are community-led gatherings of OpenShift and Kubernetes enthusiasts, and at Summit Connect they ran a "silent theater" — a quiet, conversation-first space where you could meet people without the noise of a plenary.

![The HCS company Tech Tribes networking booth at Summit Connect Utrecht.](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-nl-2026-people/rhsc-nl-2026-people-hcs-booth.jpg){ loading="lazy" decoding="async" }

On the wall beside the booth was a real customer story: the **Sittard-Geelen OpenShift** deployment — a municipal/healthcare-scale move onto OpenShift, the sort of regional public-sector adoption that is the quiet backbone of the Red Hat community in the Netherlands. These are the stories that matter more than any single keynote: proof that the platform works in the places that actually run the country.

## A name on a badge

The best conversations at a conference start with a badge. One I kept coming back to: **Jeroen Baten, Coderial BV**. A great example of the small, sharp engineering companies that keep the OpenShift ecosystem moving — the people who ship the tooling and the integrations that make the platform useful day to day.

![A conference badge — Jeroen Baten, Coderial BV — at Summit Connect Utrecht.](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-nl-2026-people/rhsc-nl-2026-people-badge.jpg){ loading="lazy" decoding="async" }

## Pull the V…

And because it is the Netherlands, there was, of course, a Dutch snack vending machine. The "Pull the V…" machine dispenses the national snack — the *kroket*: rundvleeskroket, oesterzwamkroket, and the usual suspects. No Dutch conference is complete without a wall of them.

![A Dutch snack vending machine — "Pull the V…" — at Summit Connect Utrecht.](/blog/events/red-hat-summit-connect-nl-2026-people/rhsc-nl-2026-people-snacks.jpg){ loading="lazy" decoding="async" }

The community layer is the part of a conference you cannot see in the slides — and it is the part that decides whether the technology you leave with actually gets built.
