Space is the most unforgiving environment electronics can face — bathed in radiation, impossible to repair, and expected to work flawlessly for decades. So it is a striking endorsement that space agencies and satellite makers are increasingly choosing **RISC-V**. The headline: **NASA selected a RISC-V design for its next-generation spaceflight computer**. Here is why the open ISA is heading off-world.

![Specialized RISC-V silicon on display at the Summit](/blog/events/riscv-summit-2026/riscv-summit-56.jpg)

## Why Space Computing Is Special

Spacecraft electronics face constraints unlike anything on the ground:

- **Radiation** — cosmic rays and charged particles flip bits (single-event upsets) and cause permanent damage.
- **No repairs** — once launched, hardware must self-detect and recover from faults autonomously.
- **Extreme longevity** — missions run for 10–20+ years, so the architecture and software must remain supportable for decades.
- **Tight power and thermal budgets** — every watt counts on a satellite.

Traditionally these needs were met with expensive, proprietary, and often technologically *dated* radiation-hardened processors. RISC-V offers a fresh path.

## NASA's HPSC: The Landmark Win

The flagship example is NASA's **High-Performance Spaceflight Computing (HPSC)** processor. NASA selected a **RISC-V-based design**, developed with **SiFive** and **Microchip**, to be the workhorse compute platform for future missions. The goal is a leap in onboard processing power — for autonomous navigation, on-board AI, and data handling — over the decades-old chips that still fly today.

That a risk-averse agency like NASA chose RISC-V for mission-critical compute is a powerful signal of the ISA's maturity and the strength of its [ecosystem](/blog/risc-v-ecosystem-2026-vendors-profiles-momentum/).

## Why RISC-V Fits Space So Well

The same qualities that win in [automotive](/blog/risc-v-automotive-functional-safety/) and sovereignty apply, amplified:

- **Customization** — designers can add fault-tolerance features (redundancy, error detection) directly into an open core, rather than accepting whatever a vendor offers.
- **No single-source / export risk** — space programs are wary of proprietary, export-restricted IP from a single supplier; an open ISA with multiple implementers reduces that risk (a clear [sovereignty](/blog/risc-v-european-digital-sovereignty/) benefit for national programs).
- **Architectural stability** — RISC-V's frozen base ISA protects software investments across multi-decade missions.
- **Auditability** — for the highest-assurance systems, being able to inspect the [core design](/blog/risc-v-open-source-cores-cva6-xiangshan/) itself is invaluable.

## Radiation Hardening and Fault Tolerance

An open ISA does not magically survive radiation — that is an *implementation* property, achieved with established techniques layered onto a RISC-V core:

- **Lockstep / redundant cores** — run computations in duplicate (or triplicate) and vote on results to catch faults, building on the same mechanisms used for [functional safety](/blog/risc-v-automotive-functional-safety/).
- **ECC everywhere** — error-correcting codes on memories and buses to catch and fix bit flips.
- **Triple-modular redundancy (TMR)** — critical logic replicated three times with majority voting.
- **Rad-hard processes** — special manufacturing to resist permanent damage.
- **Watchdogs and scrubbing** — continuously check and refresh memory to prevent accumulated errors.

Because RISC-V cores are open and customizable, these protections can be designed *into* the silicon precisely where a mission needs them.

## NewSpace and Small Satellites

It is not only flagship agency missions. The **NewSpace** boom — constellations of small, cheaper satellites — is a natural fit for RISC-V's low cost and flexibility. CubeSats and smallsats can use commercial RISC-V parts with mission-appropriate fault tolerance, getting capable compute without the price tag of traditional rad-hard chips. The same [embedded](/blog/risc-v-embedded-iot-microcontroller-guide/) strengths that win on Earth apply in orbit.

## On-Board AI in Orbit

A growing driver is **edge AI in space**: processing imagery and sensor data on the satellite instead of downlinking everything. That demands far more compute than legacy space processors provide — exactly the gap HPSC and RISC-V [AI acceleration](/blog/risc-v-ai-accelerators-open-silicon-2026/) aim to fill. Smarter satellites that decide what data is worth sending home need powerful, efficient, customizable silicon.

## The Bottom Line

Space is the ultimate proving ground, and RISC-V is rising to it. **NASA's HPSC** processor — RISC-V-based, built with SiFive and Microchip — is the landmark, but the logic applies across the industry: an open, customizable, stable ISA lets designers build in the **radiation hardening and fault tolerance** that missions demand, free of single-source and export risk, with software supportable for decades. From flagship probes to NewSpace constellations doing AI in orbit, open silicon is going to space.
