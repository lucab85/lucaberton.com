On Thursday 7 August 2025 I attended **World RISC-V Days** in Hanoi, Vietnam. The slide on the screen said it all: "Thursday, August 7, 2:00pm - 4:00pm, Hanoi University of Science and Technology", with the university's School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering as the host named at the top. The session was held in the university's large conference hall, and the programme was a run of short talks, each on a different corner of the RISC-V world: a company building products, a security talk, a university research institute, a privacy company betting on open hardware, and a closing talk on AI and chip design.

I was there as an attendee. I'm not on the agenda, and no slide lists me as a speaker. I work with [DeepComputing](/blog/deepcomputing-dc-roma-riscv-deepseek-local-ai-2026/) on RISC-V, so the first talk was the one I was most curious about, but I took photos of every talk and this post follows them in order.

![Luca Berton seated in the hall at World RISC-V Days Hanoi, with the event title slide on the screen](/blog/events/risc-v-world-days-hanoi-2025/hall-world-risc-v-days-slide.jpg)

*The title slide before the first talk: World RISC-V Days, Hanoi, Vietnam, 2:00pm to 4:00pm.*

## DeepComputing's RISC-V Innovation Journey

The first talk was titled **"DeepComputing's RISC-V Innovation Journey"** and the slide is dated July 2025. The name card on the table in front of the speaker's seat reads "Dr. Yuning Liang", "Founder" for Deep Computing. The slide's own text names him as the presenter.

![The DeepComputing title slide, July 2025, on the screen at the front of the hall while the speaker stands with a microphone](/blog/events/risc-v-world-days-hanoi-2025/deepcomputing-innovation-journey.jpg)

*DeepComputing's "RISC-V Innovation Journey" title slide.*

DeepComputing's website describes the company as building RISC-V computing hardware. The DC-ROMA line includes mainboards compatible with the Framework Laptop 13, plus RISC-V laptops and tablets. I'd already seen the same team at their booth in Amsterdam a few weeks later, which I wrote up in the [Open Source Summit Europe 2025 recap](/blog/open-source-summit-europe-2025-amsterdam-recap/). For more on their hardware, see [the DC-ROMA post](/blog/deepcomputing-dc-roma-riscv-deepseek-local-ai-2026/) and [OpenBao on RISC-V](/blog/openbao-risc-v-deepcomputing-open-hardware-2026/).

## Applications of RISC-V SoC in enhancing security of embedded systems

The second talk was **"Applications of RISC-V SoC in enhancing security of embedded system"**, presented by **Dr. Dao Ba Anh** of the **Academy of Cryptography Techniques**, as named on the title slide, which is dated 8/7/2025 and tagged "RISC-V Days Hanoi 2025".

![The title slide of the embedded security talk with the presenter at the lectern and the audience seated at the conference tables](/blog/events/risc-v-world-days-hanoi-2025/soc-security-embedded-title.jpg)

*The embedded-security talk from the Academy of Cryptography Techniques.*

Its "Motivation" slide compared three instruction set architectures side by side:

- **x86** as a closed ISA, found in most desktops, laptops and servers with an Intel or AMD processor.
- **ARM** as a closed ISA, used in Android and iOS devices and in newer Apple computers.
- **RISC-V** as an open ISA, where, in the slide's words, anybody can design and sell a RISC-V processor without constraints on their actions.

A column on the right listed what "open" means in practice: an open instruction set architecture, modular design with optional extensions, open-source processing cores and open-source SoC design frameworks. If you want the background, I explain the idea in [What is RISC-V](/blog/what-is-risc-v-open-isa-explained/).

![The Motivation slide comparing x86, ARM and RISC-V instruction set architectures as closed or open ISAs](/blog/events/risc-v-world-days-hanoi-2025/isa-motivation-slide.jpg)

*The "Motivation" slide: x86 and ARM as closed ISAs, RISC-V as an open one.*

## RISC-V research at VNU-ITI

Next came **"RISC-V Ecosystem Research and Development Activities at VNU-ITI"** from the **VNU Information Technology Institute**, part of Vietnam National University, Hanoi. The slide credits Manh-Hiep Dao, Duy-Hieu Bui and Xuan-Tu Tran of the Research Center for Integrated Circuits & Applications (CICA), and shows chip photos and a layout. I can't tell from the slide which of the three authors was at the microphone, so I won't guess.

![The VNU-ITI title slide with chip images, and the presenter at the front of the room](/blog/events/risc-v-world-days-hanoi-2025/vnu-iti-title.jpg)

*The VNU-ITI title slide: RISC-V ecosystem research and development activities.*

A later slide listed the institute's collaborations, grouped into universities and research institutions, industrial collaborators and societies. The logos on it included Toshiba, Avnet, Keysight, Synopsys, Muse Semiconductor, TSMC and GlobalFoundries, plus several European and Asian universities and the IEEE Circuits and Systems Society.

![A collaborations slide with university, industry and society logos, with the presenter standing at the lectern](/blog/events/risc-v-world-days-hanoi-2025/vnu-iti-collaborations.jpg)

*The collaborations slide from the VNU-ITI talk.*

## Open Hardware: Not If, But When

The title that stuck with me was **"Open Hardware: Not If, But When"**, subtitled "Why ExpressVPN is Betting on RISC-V for Tomorrow's Privacy". The slide names **Peter Membrey**, Chief Research Officer, and carries the ExpressVPN logo and a RISC-V Strategic Member badge.

![The Open Hardware: Not If, But When title slide with the ExpressVPN logo and the speaker standing in front of it](/blog/events/risc-v-world-days-hanoi-2025/open-hardware-not-if-but-when.jpg)

*"Open Hardware: Not If, But When", with the ExpressVPN and RISC-V Strategic Member logos.*

My take: the privacy argument for open hardware is the same as for open source software. If you can't inspect the silicon, you're trusting the vendor for the layer underneath all your other controls. That's also why I find RISC-V interesting for [secrets management](/blog/openbao-risc-v-deepcomputing-open-hardware-2026/) and sovereign infrastructure.

## From AI-generated chip design to emerging trends

The last talk I photographed was **"RISC-V's Growing Impact: From AI-Generated Chip Design to Emerging Trends in Industry"**, with the line "Presenter: Lan Dang, World RISC-V Days - Hanoi, Vietnam" on the title slide. A later slide quoted a newcomer engineer who was new to RISC-V and chip design and felt astonished by its progress and innovation.

![The RISC-V title slide for the talk on AI-generated chip design, with the presenter reading at the lectern](/blog/events/risc-v-world-days-hanoi-2025/ai-chip-design-trends.jpg)

*The closing talk on AI-generated chip design and industry trends.*

## What I took away

Looking back at the photos, the mix of a hardware vendor, a cryptography academy, a research institute and a privacy company in a two-hour session is a good picture of how broad RISC-V has become: products, security, research and privacy all in one room.
