On 3 November 2026 I spent a day at **SreDay DASH0** — an intimate SRE day held in a loft, run around **dash0**, the observability company whose tagline is *beyond observability, ship more, break less*. No main stage, no expo floor: leather sofas, exposed ceiling, and a projector. The kind of format where the talks land, because the room is small enough that every question is heard.

The through-line of the day was the one I keep coming back to on the observability side of the platform: **OpenTelemetry has matured into a toolbox, and the interesting work now is in instrumentation and cost.**

## OpenTelemetry is a toolbox, not a single tool

The first framing was the one that finally made OTEL click for a lot of people: the OpenTelemetry ecosystem is large, and "OpenTelemetry" is not one product. On your services side you have the OTel API and the OTel SDKs, plus automatic instrumentation. On the shared-infrastructure side you have Kubernetes, service meshes and cloud services. The client side is browsers, mobile and managed databases. All of it funnels through one thing — the **OTel Collector**, which receives, processes and exports telemetry — and out the other end goes Prometheus (metrics), Jaeger (traces), and dash0 (all signals, and a shared pipeline). The point of the ecosystem, and the reason it wins, is that the pipeline is the standard: change the back end, keep the front end.

## eBPF changes the instrumentation game

The most concrete shift on the day was **OBI — OpenTelemetry eBPF Instrumentation**. The headline slide was the one I'd have put on my own deck: *you add nothing to your image, because it monitors from outside.* OBI reads protocol-level distributed tracing (HTTP, gRPC, SQL, Redis, Kafka) straight off the sockets, so the language doesn't matter — Go, Rust, C++, a vendor binary you can't rebuild, a database. What it costs: elevated privileges (it needs a DaemonSet to control the host), and sharp edges in trace-context propagation (in Go it follows goroutine ancestry six levels deep, and TLS can break the injected context). Status on the day: beta at KubeCon EU 2026, **1.0 shipped 30 October 2026**.

Then the caveat that separates a demo from production: the **OpenTelemetry Operator** gives you zero-code, automatic injection on Kubernetes — *but you still have to know your libc.* Alpine links against musl, the default build expects glibc, and the wrong value gives you zero spans. The profilers never load. The lesson is the same one I make on the platform side: *zero code* does not mean *zero knowledge*.

## The economics of telemetry

The part that matters most to a platform team is cost, and dash0's **SignalControl** is exactly that lens: a pipeline that reduces telemetry volume, cuts ingestion cost and retains only the data you need — filter and convert rules across logs, metrics and spans, with a live picture of what is ingested versus stored. On the day the numbers were telling: tens of thousands of events ingested, a few thousand stored, with per-signal reduction ratios. That is the observability analogue of the cost-control pillar in my [From AI Demo to Production](/blog/ai-tech-summit-skopje-2026-confidentialmind-built-twice/) framework: the biggest lever is not the price of a token or a data point, it is *utilisation* — and knowing what you actually need to keep.

Alongside it: **dash0's own semantic conventions** (a `dash0-metrics.yaml` of the metrics dash0 itself emits — data points ingested, time series, counters and gauges), and the newer **AI SLO insights** and **Automerge** views, where the platform starts reasoning about service-level objectives rather than just plotting them.

## And the pub quiz

No SRE day is complete without one. SreDay DASH0 closed with an **OTel Pub Quiz** — register via a QR code, random teams, and a scoring system built from poker chips stamped *beyond observability / ship more / break less*, in fives and tens. It was the right note to end on: the serious engineering of the morning, and the reason the community keeps showing up, in the same room.
