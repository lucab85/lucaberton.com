**TalosCon 2026** is the user conference for [Talos Linux](https://www.siderolabs.com/talos-linux/), the immutable Kubernetes operating system, and it runs today and tomorrow (15 and 16 October) at the **Hotel Okura** in Amsterdam, hosted by Sidero Labs. [Last year's edition](/blog/taloscon-2025-amsterdam-recap/) had 280 people in a converted venue on Generaal Vetterstraat; this year the Grand Ballroom was full before the first keynote.

These are my notes from the morning: Steve Francis's opening, Talos 1.14, Talos Containers and Talos Enterprise Linux, the Talos Hypervisor demo, and the Yardi session on Talos Director. Where I quote a number, it is what was on the slide. I'll cover the afternoon breakouts and the sovereign cloud panel in a separate post.

## Steve Francis: the bet from 2019, and the Yardi question

Steve Francis, President of Sidero, opened with the mission slide: *build the next generation of distributed systems*, "intrinsically secure software that lets every infrastructure operation be automated, so customers can focus on their business, not IT". In 2019 that meant starting where the complexity, and the value, is greatest: an operating system for Kubernetes.

![Steve Francis on stage with the mission slide: build the next generation of distributed systems](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-steve-francis-mission.jpg)

The 2019 bet was five rules that still define Talos: **no shell, no SSH, no package manager, an API for everything, immutable**. "In 2019 that was a strange idea, and plenty of people told us so."

![The 2019 bet: an operating system for machines, not humans, with the five rules](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-os-for-machines-not-humans.jpg)

The engineering velocity chart was the number I'll remember. Headline features per minor release went from **2** in v0.9 to **19** in 1.0, **25** in 1.8, **29** in 1.13 and **44** in 1.14, counting the headline items in each release's notes and excluding component updates.

![New features per Talos minor release, from 2 in v0.9 to 44 in 1.14](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-features-per-release.jpg)

"Just the companies speaking here today" is a slide that says a lot about where Talos is now: Nokia, DHL, Norsk Helsenett, Founda Health, PostFinance, DE-CIX, OpenResearch, Enovis, STACKIT, VSHN, Exoawk, ReveCom and Atlas Consulting. As the caption put it: banks, health systems, internet exchanges, logistics and sovereign clouds run Talos.

![Just the companies speaking here today: Nokia, DHL, Norsk Helsenett, PostFinance, DE-CIX and more](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-companies-speaking.jpg)

Then the slide everyone was waiting for. Sidero Labs is now part of **Yardi**, the property management software company, and Francis addressed the two objections head-on: *"You're too small, and you might go out of business"* and *"What if you get acquired, and they kill the product or change the license?"* Both got a green tick under "always raised, now gone".

![Being part of a large enterprise has its advantages: the two objections, answered](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-yardi-objections.jpg)

**Anant Yardi**, President of Yardi Systems, took the stage himself. His slide: *one immutable, open-source OS for every workload*, containers, Kubernetes and virtual machines (via Talos Hypervisor) on Talos Linux, with one API, one audit trail and the MPL 2.0 licence. Yardi runs Talos in production, and the message was that the licence is staying open.

![Anant Yardi with the slide: one immutable, open-source OS for every workload](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-one-os-every-workload.jpg)

## Sterling Koch: Talos 1.14, the biggest release ever

Sterling Koch, Senior Director of Product, walked through **Talos 1.14** with a "until 1.14 → in 1.14" slide for each change. The release has **623 changes** and **45 release-note sections**, against a previous record of 30. For comparison, 1.10 to 1.13 had between 279 and 367 commits each.

![Talos 1.14, the biggest Talos release ever: 623 changes and 45 release-note sections](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-talos-114-biggest-release.jpg)

The seven changes, in the order he presented them:

**1. Big config → small documents.** The single machine config with `machine:` and `cluster:` sections is split into small documents: `UserVolumeConfig`, `LinkConfig`, `KernelModuleConfig`, `KubeAPIServerConfig`, `KubeletConfig`, `BGPInstanceConfig` and more.

![Big config becomes small documents in Talos 1.14](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-config-documents.jpg)

**2. Automatic reboots → config applies live.** Until 1.14, `talosctl apply-config` checked a "live-apply list" and rebooted the machine for anything not on it. In 1.14 the config applies live and the machine stays up.

![Automatic reboots become live config apply in Talos 1.14](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-config-applies-live.jpg)

**3. Shared space → K8s in a box.** The container runtime, the kubelet and the pods now run inside a **sandboxd** boundary on the machine instead of sharing space with Talos core. This is the "workload isolation" item from the 44-feature list.

![Shared space becomes Kubernetes in a box: container runtime, kubelet and pods inside sandboxd](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-k8s-in-a-box.jpg)

**4. Basic disks → RAID and LVM.** Talos can now boot from a RAID 1 mirror, "boots from either disk", instead of Talos on disk 1 and data on disk 2.

![Basic disks become RAID and LVM: Talos boots from either disk of a RAID 1 mirror](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-raid-lvm.jpg)

**5. Bolt-on BGP → built-in BGP.** A Talos node can now announce its own address (10.0.0.10 in the example) to two upstream switches natively, where before you needed a BGP add-on.

![Bolt-on BGP becomes built-in BGP: a Talos node announcing itself to two switches](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-built-in-bgp.jpg)

**6. Secure → even more secure.** On when you upgrade: authenticated time sync, TLS 1.3 for etcd and the Kubernetes API server, encrypted support bundles, no more ICMP redirects. Yours to turn on: encrypted DNS.

![Secure becomes even more secure: the security changes that are on when you upgrade](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-even-more-secure.jpg)

**7. Kubernetes only → your containers too.** This is **Talos Containers**, new in 1.14: a `ContainerConfig` document runs your container on the machine next to Kubernetes, or without Kubernetes at all.

![Kubernetes only becomes your containers too: ContainerConfig in Talos 1.14](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-talos-containers.jpg)

Who it's for: the edge box that runs one service, the appliance where a cluster is overkill, and "the VM you've been meaning to replace". The slide quoted Kevin Tijssen, Solutions Architect at Sidero: "Talos Containers works amazing. I'm already converting my old Debian VMs to this."

![Who Talos Containers is for: the edge box, the appliance, the VM you've been meaning to replace](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-containers-who-its-for.jpg)

### Talos Enterprise Linux

The second half of Koch's talk was commercial: **Talos Enterprise Linux**, "production guarantees, same open-source code". One commercial licence, one support relationship, the same codebase as Talos Linux (MPL 2.0). It answers four questions security reviews ask:

![Common security questions: what's in this build, is the cryptography FIPS validated, CVE response, IP claims](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-security-questions.jpg)

1. **What's in this build, and can you prove where it came from?** A per-build **SBOM** including your extensions, and a curated **VEX** that says which CVEs affect each Talos release and why the rest don't ("not built", "never runs"). The scale: **5,681 Linux kernel CVEs in 2025**, including Bluetooth, which Talos doesn't build.

![What's in this build: SBOM and VEX, with 5,681 Linux kernel CVEs in 2025](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-sbom-vex.jpg)

   Plus a **signed attestation** from the image Sidero builds to the image you boot: "verify that the image you booted is the one we built".

![Proof of where it came from: signed attestation from the image Sidero builds to the one you boot](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-signed-attestation.jpg)

2. **Is the cryptography FIPS validated?** FIPS-enabled builds use the Go Cryptographic Module, validated under **FIPS 140-3**, certificate **CMVP #5247**.

![FIPS-enabled builds: Go Cryptographic Module, FIPS 140-3, CMVP #5247](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-fips-builds.jpg)

3. **When a CVE drops, what's your response commitment?** Defined CVE response times by severity, support from people who know Talos, upgrade and migration assistance.
4. **If someone makes an IP claim against this code, who's on the hook?** Commercial IP indemnity.

Who it's for, on the slide: security reviews that ask those four questions, regulated industries, and **NIS2 and Cyber Resilience Act** work. If you're doing [CRA compliance on a Kubernetes platform](/blog/cra-kubernetes-platform-compliance/), the SBOM-plus-VEX part is the piece most vendors still don't ship.

He closed with three things to do: upgrade to Talos 1.14, try Talos Containers (Maja Bojarska's session at 15:30), and talk to Sidero about Talos Enterprise Linux.

![Three things to do: upgrade to Talos 1.14, try Talos Containers, talk about Talos Enterprise Linux](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-three-things-to-do.jpg)

## Justin Garrison: the Talos Hypervisor, live

Justin Garrison's session was almost entirely a live demo, with a small bare-metal box on the lectern. The dashboard showed **Talos v1.15.0-alpha.0** running as a hypervisor host, `hv1`, with 12 cores, 31 GiB of RAM and Secure Boot off for the demo.

![The Talos dashboard on the demo box: v1.15.0-alpha.0, host hv1, 12 cores, 31 GiB RAM](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-hypervisor-dashboard.jpg)

A virtual machine is a Talos document like everything else. The one he applied was a `VirtualMachineConfig` named `talos-1`: 2 CPUs, 4 GiB of memory, UEFI firmware, a 20 GiB qcow2 system disk on the default pool, provisioned from a `nocloud-amd64.qcow2` image in the default library.

![The VirtualMachineConfig document for talos-1: 2 CPUs, 4 GiB, UEFI, a 20 GiB qcow2 disk](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-vm-yaml.jpg)

The second half joined the two worlds. He watched `talosctl get virtualmachinestatus` in one pane and created a cluster in **Omni** in the other, with Talos 1.14.2 and Kubernetes 1.35 selected and 1.15.0-alpha builds visible in the version list. The VM booted Talos and joined the cluster, so the box went from hypervisor to Kubernetes node in one session.

![Creating a cluster in Omni while watching virtualmachinestatus from talosctl](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-omni-create-cluster.jpg)

In the [press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/talos-linux-adds-native-hypervisor-and-edge-container-support--see-it-live-at-taloscon-2026-302877040.html) Sidero says the Hypervisor alpha is what's shown at TalosCon, with general availability in December 2026, and that it's fully open source. VMs don't need Kubernetes, which is the point for the "VM you've been meaning to replace" from Koch's slide.

## Yardi: Talos Director, a vCenter alternative in production

Anant Yardi returned with **Neil Levien**, Director of Cloud Services at Yardi, for *Talos Director, Talos Cupar*.

![Talos Director, Talos Cupar: Anant Yardi and Neil Levien](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-talos-director-title.jpg)

**Talos Director** is "a vCenter alternative, in production at Yardi": live migration, high availability, autoscaling, and network microsegmentation and firewalling at scale. It's **now in Limited Availability**; Sidero's [announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/sidero-labs-launches-talos-director-a-resource-management-platform-for-data-centers-302893616.html) puts general availability in January 2027, with Yardi already running thousands of VMs and hundreds of Kubernetes clusters on it.

![Talos Director: a vCenter alternative in production at Yardi, now in Limited Availability](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-talos-director-limited-availability.jpg)

Levien's demo was in Yardi's own production console, against real hosts. The VM detail page for `pca000a113` showed what makes this different from a hobby project: a NIC on `pct-vms-1000` (VLAN 1000) attached through an **eBPF** bridge, the physical path from the virtual NIC to a `bond2` LACP bond with two 25 Gbps members on named switch ports, and a "Runtime Firewall Status" with manager-proxied eBPF inspection on the host. Traffic shaping is a button on the NIC.

![Talos Director VM detail: eBPF bridge, LACP bond with two 25 Gbps members, runtime firewall status](/blog/events/taloscon-2026/taloscon-2026-director-vm-network.jpg)

He also walked through the dashboard, performance graphs, storage pools, a VM console and the project list. Everything on screen was Yardi infrastructure, which is a stronger demo than a staged one.

I wrote a [VMware to OpenShift Virtualization migration guide](/blog/vmware-to-openshift-virtualization-migration-guide/) last week; Talos Director is the other credible answer to the same question for teams that already run Talos. The approaches differ (KubeVirt on OpenShift against a hypervisor built into the OS), and the VMware exit is the demand behind both.

## My take

Three things stood out from the morning.

**Talos is no longer only a Kubernetes OS.** With Containers in 1.14 and the Hypervisor coming in 1.15, the same immutable machine runs a single container, a VM or a cluster, all through the same API and config documents. That is the pitch for the edge box and the appliance, and it removes the main reason people kept a Debian VM around.

**The 1.14 changes are the ones operators asked for.** Live config apply without reboots, boot from RAID 1 and built-in BGP are not flashy, but they are what you need before you put Talos in a datacenter instead of a lab. The feature chart, 2 per release to 44, shows how much faster the project moves since last year.

**The acquisition changed the conversation.** The two objections Francis listed are the ones I hear in every procurement discussion about a small vendor. Yardi on stage, Talos Director running Yardi's own cloud, and the MPL 2.0 licence on the keynote slide are a direct answer. Talos Enterprise Linux, with SBOM, VEX, FIPS 140-3 and IP indemnity, is the other half: the paperwork regulated buyers need. Whether the community keeps the same pace under a large owner is the question for next year's TalosCon.
