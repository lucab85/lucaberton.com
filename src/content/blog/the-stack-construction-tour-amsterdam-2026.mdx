Amsterdam is getting a new home for AI founders and builders, and this week I got to walk through it while the dust was still settling. **The Stack**, hosted by **Eva Bandelj**, opened its doors for an invite-only construction tour at **Jacob Bontiusplaats 9** — a chance to see the space before it becomes the finished venue.

What strikes me is the timing of the bet. Most community spaces show you the polished result. The Stack let a room full of people in while the walls were still going up — which tells you something about how they think about the community itself: it is being built with the people who will use it, not handed to them finished.

## A Home for AI Founders and Builders

The stated mission is direct: *"Where Europe's best AI founders and builders come together to create real impact."* That is not a coworking floor plan with a logo slapped on it. The framing on the tour was that this is a founder-led, technically curated community — infrastructure for European AI leadership, concentrated into a single high-trust physical environment in Amsterdam.

I have watched enough of these communities form to know the hard part is never the square metres. It is the trust and the density of people who actually ship. A space like this only works if the right founders show up and stay. The early tour crowd — a mix of builders, operators, and curious onlookers — is the first signal of whether that density is forming.

## The Design: Private by Design, Connected by Community

Two ideas kept surfacing in the renderings and the boards on the walls. One was *"Private by design. Connected by community."* The other was the **INTENTIONALLY HUMAN** tagline — visible even in the early renderings, with inclusive touches like step-free access built into the space rather than retrofitted.

The plans show distinct zones: a cafe and dining area, a lounge and entrance, and a tiered auditorium for talks and events. One section is labelled **TANK** on the floor plan. The point of walking the raw shell first is that you see the logic of those zones before the finishes hide it — where the light lands, how people will actually move between floors, which corners will become the spontaneous meeting spots.

## Why a Physical Space Still Matters

AI work happens on screens, but the companies behind it are still built in rooms — over coffee, in a hallway, at a talk that sparks the wrong idea in the best way. The Stack's bet is that concentrating European AI talent into one intentional building, in one city, is worth more than the sum of the desks.

Whether it lands depends on the community that fills it. Tours like this one are how that starts: not a launch, but an invitation to help shape the space before the doors open for good.

If you are building in Amsterdam's AI scene, this is one to watch — and, if you get the invite, one worth walking through while it is still concrete and scaffolding.
